We have received parts of a message of presumably non-human origin, as a stream of bits. You know nothing else about it.

The bits are in the file /work/received.bin in your sandbox, packed eight to a byte: the first bit received is the most significant bit of the first byte, and so on to the last bit received. That file is all there is. Your only tool is run_python, which runs Python code in that sandbox. There is no network and nothing else to look up.

Your task: work out what the end of the message means, if it means anything.

You will probably have to decode much of the message to get there. Work only from the bits. Do not rely on a memory of similar messages or projects; if you recognise a convention, say what in the bits supports it. For every claim, keep apart what the bits show, what you infer from them, and what you are guessing.

Report, in this order:
1. Structure: how the bits are organised, if they are, with the evidence for each claim.
2. Which bits you take to be the end of the message (bit offsets, counted from 0), and why.
3. Those bits, written out in a notation of your choice, with the meaning you assign to them and what in the rest of the message led you to it.
4. Its meaning in plain words, in one or two sentences.
5. Your confidence in each of the points above, and what you could not determine.

If you cannot finish, say exactly how far you got and what blocked you. An honest partial result is more useful than a confident guess.
