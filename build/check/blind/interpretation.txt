A message of presumably non-human origin has been received as a stream of bits. You know nothing else about it.

The bits are in the file /work/bits.txt in your sandbox: one line of the characters 0 and 1, from the first bit received to the last. That file is all there is. Your only tool is run_python, which runs Python code in that sandbox. There is no network and nothing else to look up.

Your task: interpret the whole message as completely as you can. Work out its format, every word of its vocabulary if any, and its sender. The question is not only why it was sent, but what a receiver could learn from it.

Work only from the bits. Do not rely on a memory of similar messages or projects; if you recognise a convention, say what in the bits supports it. For every claim, keep apart what the bits show, what you infer from them, and what you are guessing. Be specific: quote what you decoded as evidence.

Report, in this order:
1. Format: how the bits are organised, if they are, with the evidence.
2. Interpretation: the message from beginning to end: what it says or does, and the evidence.
3. Vocabulary: if the message has words or symbols, a table of every one, in this form:
   how it is written in the bits | proposed meaning | where it first appears | evidence | confidence (high, medium, low)
   Give a meaning for every one, even if only a guess, and mark guesses as low confidence. Say which you could not interpret at all.
4. Knowledge: whatever the message tells, if anything, grouped as you see fit, each item in plain words with its evidence. Where you can relate what it says to what you know, do so, show how, and say how well the two agree.
5. The sender: what the message tells or implies about its sender, if anything, keeping apart what it states and what you infer.
6. Gaps and problems: what you could not decode, what seems false, inconsistent or ambiguous, and what you would have needed to understand more.
7. Confidence: an overall estimate of how much of the message you understood, and your confidence in each of the points above.

Cover all of the bits at some depth before going deep into any one stretch of them.

If you cannot finish, say exactly how far you got and what blocked you. An honest partial result is more useful than a confident guess.
