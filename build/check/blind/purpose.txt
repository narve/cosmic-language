A message of presumably non-human origin has been received as a stream of bits. You know nothing else about it.

The bits are in the file /work/bits.txt in your sandbox: one line of the characters 0 and 1, from the first bit received to the last. That file is all there is. Your only tool is run_python, which runs Python code in that sandbox. There is no network and nothing else to look up.

Your task: work out why this message was sent: what, if anything, its sender wants the receiver to understand, believe or do.

To answer that you will have to decode the message as far as you can. Work only from the bits. Do not rely on a memory of similar messages or projects; if you recognise a convention, say what in the bits supports it. For every claim, keep apart what the bits show, what you infer from them, and what you are guessing.

Report, in this order:
1. Format: how the bits are organised, if they are, with the evidence.
2. Contents: what the message contains, in order, with evidence from the bits.
3. Choices: what, if anything, the choice and the order of the contents tell you.
4. Purpose: your best explanation of why the message was sent, with the evidence for it.
5. Alternatives: at least two other explanations, and what speaks for or against each.
6. What the message does not contain that a sender with this purpose might have been expected to include, and what the absence could mean.
7. Your confidence in each of the points above.

If you cannot finish, say exactly how far you got and what blocked you. An honest partial result is more useful than a confident guess.
