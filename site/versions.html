Four Versions

The message comes in three versions, and a fourth made for fun. The three are made from the same lessons, by leaving whole lessons out; nothing is rewritten. They differ in how much they say about who sends them and where from.

Full

Every lesson, including those that say where the message comes from: our star and our planet, and later where they are in the Galaxy and who we are. It reveals as much as possible.

Cautious

Everything except the lessons that reveal where and who we are: these are marked optional, and the build checks that no other lesson depends on them. Mathematics, logic, physics, chemistry, stars in general, the background radiation and the game are all there; our Sun and our Earth are not.

Minimal

Only the game and what it needs: how the signal is read, arithmetic, truth values, lists, quantifiers, sequences, chance and if, and then the prisoner's dilemma and the lesson peace wins, which ends with the statement that the strategy which opens in peace and answers in kind loses least. No lesson in it uses a symbol introduced outside it, and the build checks that.

Its lessons

Decoy

The full message, with the lessons on the Sun, on the Sun in the Galaxy and on pulsars replaced by lessons about Kepler-1647, a pair of stars 1.2 kiloparsecs away with a giant planet in their habitable zone, under the same names, and the lesson on humans by one on invented senders who live on a moon of its planet. It is made for fun, to see whether a blind reader places the sender there: from so far away the Galaxy's directions and the pulsars change too. Its measured ranges are checked by the build like those of the full message, but it says false things about the sender, so it is not meant to be sent. Why this system was chosen, and what changes, is on the decoy page.

The replacing lessons: the Sun (decoy), the Sun in the Galaxy (decoy) and pulsars (decoy).

A note on numbering

The symbols of each type are numbered 1, 2, 3 … in the order they first appear, and the lessons are ordered so that this holds in every version: a shorter version has no gaps, it simply stops earlier. A reader of an earlier minimal version, which did have gaps, took one for a deliberate signal; the build now checks every version for this. Elements are the exception: their number is their number of protons, so after krypton (36) the next ones in the message are xenon (54) and radon (86).
