the chemistry of life

The molecules of the lesson on common molecules are found all over the Galaxy. The ones here matter to us in particular: the air of our planet, and the molecules that life on it, and our own bodies, are built from and run on. Each is given by how many atoms of each element it contains. Most are made of hydrogen, carbon, nitrogen and oxygen only; a few also hold phosphorus, sulfur or iron.

Our air is mostly two molecules, each made of two atoms of one element. Dinitrogen shares three pairs, dioxygen two. Free dioxygen is rare between the stars and reacts easily; on our planet it is made all the time by living things.


; dinitrogen: two nitrogen atoms sharing three pairs
(= (count nitrogen dinitrogen) 2)
(= (atoms dinitrogen) (list nitrogen nitrogen))
(= (bonds dinitrogen) (list (list 1 2 3)))
; 14 electrons, as many as carbon monoxide
(= (count electron dinitrogen) (count electron carbon-monoxide))

; dioxygen: two oxygen atoms sharing two pairs
(= (count oxygen dioxygen) 2)
(= (atoms dioxygen) (list oxygen oxygen))
(= (bonds dioxygen) (list (list 1 2 2)))
(= (count electron dioxygen) 16)

; thymine: 5 carbon, 6 hydrogen, 2 nitrogen, 2 oxygen

(= (count oxygen thymine) 2)

; guanine: 5 carbon, 5 hydrogen, 5 nitrogen, 1 oxygen
(= (count carbon guanine) 5)

(= (count nitrogen guanine) 5)
(= (count oxygen guanine) 1)

; cytosine: 4 carbon, 5 hydrogen, 3 nitrogen, 1 oxygen
(= (count carbon cytosine) 4)
(= (count hydrogen cytosine) 5)
(= (count nitrogen cytosine) 3)
(= (count oxygen cytosine) 1)

; uracil: 4 carbon, 4 hydrogen, 2 nitrogen, 2 oxygen
(= (count carbon uracil) 4)
(= (count hydrogen uracil) 4)
(= (count nitrogen uracil) 2)

A DNA molecule is two long strands, each a chain of bases held on a backbone of sugar and phosphate. strands gives them as two lists of bases. Each strand has a direction of its own, set by how its backbone is built, and each list is read in that direction. Across the two strands, each base sits opposite its partner: adenine opposite thymine, guanine opposite cytosine.


; every DNA molecule has two strands
(observed (∀ u dna (= (length (strands u)) 2)))
; and every item of a strand is one of the four bases
(observed (∀ u dna (∀ v (strands u)
  (∀ w v (∃ z (list adenine thymine guanine cytosine) (= z w))))))

; adenine pairs with thymine, and guanine with cytosine
(observed (= (partner adenine) thymine))
(observed (= (partner thymine) adenine))
(observed (= (partner guanine) cytosine))
(observed (= (partner cytosine) guanine))
; so the partner of a partner is the base itself
(∀ u (list adenine thymine guanine cytosine)
  (= (partner (partner u)) u))

The backbone alternates deoxyribose and phosphate, the group that phosphoric acid forms; it is where DNA holds its phosphorus.


; phosphoric acid: 3 hydrogen, 1 phosphorus, 4 oxygen
(= (count hydrogen phosphoric-acid) 3)
(= (count phosphorus phosphoric-acid) 1)
(= (count oxygen phosphoric-acid) 4)

The two strands run in opposite directions. So the second strand, read in its own direction, is the first strand read backward, with each base replaced by its partner. The first base of one strand faces the last base of the other.


; the strands have the same length, and the base at position z of
; the second is the partner of the base at position n + 1 − z of
; the first, where n is the length
(observed (∀ u dna (let v (item (strands u) 1) (let w (item (strands u) 2)
  (and (= (length v) (length w))
       (∀ z ℕ (implies (≤ 1 z (length v))
         (= (item w z) (partner (item v (− (+ (length v) 1) z)))))))))))
; so each molecule holds as many adenine as thymine, and as many
; guanine as cytosine
(observed (∀ u dna (= (count adenine u) (count thymine u))))
(observed (∀ u dna (= (count guanine u) (count cytosine u))))

The strands wind round each other in a double helix. rise is how far the helix goes along its axis from one base pair to the next, and twist how far it turns. Its sign tells the hand, with cross as in the lesson on spin and handedness: the DNA of all life here is a right-handed helix, apart from short stretches that can for a while take a rare left-handed form.


; the helix is right-handed: its twist is positive
(observed (∀ u dna (> (twist u) 0)))
; a full turn takes between 2³ and 13 base pairs: about 10.4
(observed (< (^ 2 3) (÷ (× 2 π) (average u dna (twist u))) 13))

; the rise is a length
(∀ u dna (= (dim (rise u)) L))
; between 2⁸³ and 2⁸⁵ Planck lengths per base pair: about 2⁸⁴
(observed (< (^ 2 83) (÷ (average u dna (rise u)) planck-length) (^ 2 85)))
; so a full turn is between 2⁸⁷ and 2⁸⁸ Planck lengths long
(observed (< (^ 2 87)
              (÷ (× (average u dna (rise u)) (÷ (× 2 π) (average u dna (twist u))))
                 planck-length)
              (^ 2 88)))
