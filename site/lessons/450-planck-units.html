Planck units

New: the constants planck-length, planck-time and planck-mass; each is defined from c, ħ and G:


; each unit is defined by its square
; planck-length² = ħ × G ÷ c³
(= (^ planck-length 2) (÷ (× ħ G) (^ c 3)))
; planck-time² = ħ × G ÷ c⁵
(= (^ planck-time 2) (÷ (× ħ G) (^ c 5)))
; planck-mass² = ħ × c ÷ G
(= (^ planck-mass 2) (÷ (× ħ c) G))

; they are a length, a time and a mass
(= (dim planck-length) L)
(= (dim planck-time) T)
(= (dim planck-mass) M)

; light travels one Planck length in one Planck time
(= planck-length (× c planck-time))

Now familiar things can be measured in these units. The ranges are deliberately rough, between two powers of 2: what matters is that the receiver recognizes them.

One new constant appears here: hydrogen-line, the photon of the 21 cm line of hydrogen, the best-known line in radio astronomy. It is identified by its energy compared with the step from level 1 to level 2, and by its period.


; the proton's mass: about 7.7 × 10⁻²⁰ Planck masses (between 1/2⁶⁴
; and 1/2⁶³)
(observed (< (÷ 1 (^ 2 64)) (÷ (mass proton) planck-mass) (÷ 1 (^ 2 63))))
; the electron's mass: about 4.2 × 10⁻²³ Planck masses (between
; 1/2⁷⁵ and 1/2⁷⁴)
(observed (< (÷ 1 (^ 2 75)) (÷ (mass electron) planck-mass) (÷ 1 (^ 2 74))))

; the 21 cm line of hydrogen: its energy is about 5.8 × 10⁻⁷ of the
; step from level 1 to level 2
; (between 1/2²¹ and 1/2²⁰)
(observed (< (÷ 1 (^ 2 21))
              (÷ (energy hydrogen-line) (− (level hydrogen 2) (level hydrogen 1)))
              (÷ 1 (^ 2 20))))

; one period of the line (1 ÷ its frequency) is about 1.3 × 10³⁴
; Planck times (between 2¹¹³ and 2¹¹⁴)
(observed (< (^ 2 113) (÷ 1 (× (frequency hydrogen-line) planck-time)) (^ 2 114)))
; so its wavelength is as many Planck lengths: about 21 cm
(observed (< (^ 2 113) (÷ (wavelength hydrogen-line) planck-length) (^ 2 114)))

One more ratio is famous among physicists. The step from level 1 to level 2 of hydrogen, divided by the energy of the electron's mass (m × c²), is 3/8 of the square of the fine-structure constant, about 2.0 × 10⁻⁵:


; (level 2 − level 1) ÷ (electron mass × c²): about 2.0 × 10⁻⁵
; (between 1/2¹⁶ and 1/2¹⁵)
(observed (< (÷ 1 (^ 2 16))
              (÷ (− (level hydrogen 2) (level hydrogen 1)) (× (mass electron) (^ c 2)))
              (÷ 1 (^ 2 15))))
