New symbol: ^, raising to a natural-number power. Like − and ÷ it takes exactly two arguments, and the lesson shows why: grouping matters. 


; the powers of 2
(= (^ 2 1) 2)
(= (^ 2 2) 4)
(= (^ 2 3) 8)
(= (^ 2 4) 16)
(= (^ 2 5) 32)

; a power multiplies the same number again and again: 2³ = 2 × 2 × 2
(= (^ 2 3) (× 2 2 2) 8)
(= (^ 3 2) (× 3 3) 9)
(= (^ 7 2) (× 7 7) 49)

; power 1 changes nothing; 0 to any power above 0 is 0;
; power 0 gives 1
(= (^ 5 1) 5)
(= (^ 0 5) 0)
(= (^ 5 0) 1)

; 2⁸ is 256, the first number that needs two digits
(= (^ 2 8) (big 1 0))

; grouping matters: (2³)² is 64, but 2 to the power 3² is 512
(= (^ (^ 2 3) 2) 64)
(= (^ 2 (^ 3 2)) (big 2 0))
(≠ (^ (^ 2 3) 2) (^ 2 (^ 3 2)))

; the laws: u² = u × u
(∀ u ℝ (= (^ u 2) (× u u)))
; one more in the exponent is one more factor
(∀ u ℝ (∀ v ℕ (= (^ u (+ v 1)) (× u (^ u v)))))
; adding exponents multiplies the powers
(∀ u ℝ (∀ v ℕ (∀ w ℕ (= (^ u (+ v w)) (× (^ u v) (^ u w))))))

With sequence from the lesson on sequences, the powers of 2 are one infinite sequence. And apply can add up an infinite sequence: applying + to it means the value that the sums of the first items come ever closer to. 


; the powers of 2: the fourth item is 2³ = 8
(= (item (sequence u ℕ (^ 2 u)) 4) 8)

; the sums of the first items: 1/2 + 1/4 = 3/4,
; then 7/8, then 15/16, ever closer to 1
(= (apply + (sequence u (list 0 1) (÷ 1 (^ 2 (+ u 1))))) (÷ 3 4))
(= (apply + (sequence u (list 0 1 2) (÷ 1 (^ 2 (+ u 1))))) (÷ 7 8))
(= (apply + (sequence u (list 0 1 2 3) (÷ 1 (^ 2 (+ u 1))))) (÷ 15 16))

; and all of them: 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + … = 1
(= (apply + (sequence u ℕ (÷ 1 (^ 2 (+ u 1))))) 1)
