the prisoner's dilemma

New: the moves cooperate and defect, and moves, the list of the two. There are two moves, they are different, and there are no others:


; two moves, and they are not the same
(≠ cooperate defect)
; the list of the moves: it has two items
(= moves (list cooperate defect))
(= (length moves) 2)

; every move is one or the other
(∀ u moves (or (= u cooperate) (= u defect)))

Two players each pick a move, at the same moment, neither knowing what the other picks. New: loss, what the round costs a player. Its first argument is the player's own move, its second the other's. A player is better off with a smaller loss.

The game is told in losses, not in gains. Neither is more natural than the other for a reader who shares nothing with us: that less loss is better is as much something to be worked out as that more gain is. There is a practical reason too. Among humans this game has a standard table, with the numbers 0, 1, 3 and 5, and those numbers are famous. A reader that has learned from human writing recognises them at once, without reading the lesson. So the message uses other numbers: four primes in a row, 2, 3, 5 and 7. With those, a reader has to work the game out from what the message says. If such a reader cannot do that, a reader from elsewhere will not be able to either.


; both cooperate: each loses 3. Both defect: each loses 5
(= (loss cooperate cooperate) 3)
(= (loss defect defect) 5)

; one cooperates and the other defects: the one who cooperates loses
; 7, the one who defects loses 2
(= (loss cooperate defect) 7)
(= (loss defect cooperate) 2)

; what the two lose together: 6 when both cooperate, 9 when one
; exploits the other, 10 when both defect
(= (+ (loss cooperate cooperate) (loss cooperate cooperate)) 6)
(= (+ (loss cooperate defect) (loss defect cooperate)) 9)
(= (+ (loss defect defect) (loss defect defect)) 10)

This is the dilemma. Whatever the other player does, a player loses less by defecting. Yet if both reason that way, each ends up losing more than if both had cooperated.


; whatever the other does, defecting costs less
(∀ u moves (< (loss defect u) (loss cooperate u)))
; yet each loses less when both cooperate than when both defect
(< (loss cooperate cooperate) (loss defect defect))

; the four losses in order: 2, 3, 5, 7
(< (loss defect cooperate) (loss cooperate cooperate) (loss defect defect) (loss cooperate defect))
; and cooperating together costs less than taking turns to exploit
; each other: 2 × 3 is less than 2 + 7
(< (× 2 (loss cooperate cooperate)) (+ (loss defect cooperate) (loss cooperate defect)))

Strategies

A history is a list of the rounds played so far, each round a list of two moves: one's own, then the other's. (move s h) is a list to pick the next move from: a single move for a fixed choice, repeated moves for a chance.


; always-cooperate and always-defect ignore the history
(∀ u histories (= (move always-cooperate u) (list cooperate)))
(∀ u histories (= (move always-defect u) (list defect)))

; tit-for-tat: cooperate first, then do what the other did last
(= (move tit-for-tat (list)) (list cooperate))
(∀ u histories (implies (> (length u) 0)
  (= (move tit-for-tat u) (list (item (item u (length u)) 2)))))

; suspicious-tit-for-tat: the same rule, but it defects first
(= (move suspicious-tit-for-tat (list)) (list defect))
(∀ u histories (implies (> (length u) 0)
  (= (move suspicious-tit-for-tat u) (list (item (item u (length u)) 2)))))

; grim: cooperate as long as the other has never defected
(∀ u histories (implies (= (count defect (sequence v u (item v 2))) 0)
  (= (move grim u) (list cooperate))))
(∀ u histories (implies (> (count defect (sequence v u (item v 2))) 0)
  (= (move grim u) (list defect))))

; pavlov: cooperate if both did the same last time, otherwise defect
(= (move pavlov (list)) (list cooperate))
(∀ u histories (implies (and (> (length u) 0)
                                (= (item (item u (length u)) 1) (item (item u (length u)) 2)))
  (= (move pavlov u) (list cooperate))))
(∀ u histories (implies (and (> (length u) 0)
                                (≠ (item (item u (length u)) 1) (item (item u (length u)) 2)))
  (= (move pavlov u) (list defect))))

; generous-tit-for-tat: like tit-for-tat, but after a defection it
; forgives once in three
(= (move generous-tit-for-tat (list)) (list cooperate))
(∀ u histories (implies (and (> (length u) 0) (= (item (item u (length u)) 2) cooperate))
  (= (move generous-tit-for-tat u) (list cooperate))))
(∀ u histories (implies (and (> (length u) 0) (= (item (item u (length u)) 2) defect))
  (= (move generous-tit-for-tat u) (list cooperate defect defect))))

; random: whatever has happened, it picks one of the two moves by
; chance
(∀ u histories (= (move random u) (list cooperate defect)))

The same strategies with if

implies states that something holds where a condition holds; if picks a value. Two sentences with opposite conditions, like those above, say the same as one sentence with if. So the strategies are defined twice, on purpose: each form helps the receiver read the other.


; tit-for-tat: cooperate first, then do what the other did last
(∀ u histories (= (move tit-for-tat u)
  (if (= (length u) 0) (list cooperate) (list (item (item u (length u)) 2)))))

; suspicious-tit-for-tat: defect first, then do what the other did
; last
(∀ u histories (= (move suspicious-tit-for-tat u)
  (if (= (length u) 0) (list defect) (list (item (item u (length u)) 2)))))

; grim: cooperate as long as the other has never defected
(∀ u histories (= (move grim u)
  (if (= (count defect (sequence v u (item v 2))) 0) (list cooperate) (list defect))))

; pavlov: cooperate first, then cooperate if both did the same last
; time
(∀ u histories (= (move pavlov u)
  (if (= (length u) 0)
      (list cooperate)
      (if (= (item (item u (length u)) 1) (item (item u (length u)) 2)) (list cooperate) (list defect)))))

Playing a game

New: tournament, the list of the six strategies that always make a fixed choice, and the functions rounds (the history after a number of rounds) and cost (what a game costs a player in all). First the rules: each round, each player takes its strategy's move given the rounds so far, seen from its own side, which is the same game with the players swapped.


(= tournament (list always-cooperate always-defect tit-for-tat suspicious-tit-for-tat grim pavlov))
(∀ u tournament (∀ v tournament (= (rounds u v 0) (list))))
(∀ u tournament (∀ v tournament (∀ w ℕ (= (length (rounds u v w)) w))))

; earlier rounds stay as they were
(∀ u tournament (∀ v tournament (∀ w ℕ (∀ z ℕ (implies (≤ 1 z w)
  (= (item (rounds u v w) z) (item (rounds u v z) z)))))))

; the same game seen from the other side: each round with its two
; moves swapped
(∀ u tournament (∀ v tournament (∀ w ℕ (∀ z ℕ (implies (≤ 1 z w)
  (= (item (item (rounds u v w) z) 1) (item (item (rounds v u w) z) 2)))))))

; the next round: each player's move, given the game so far from its
; own side
(∀ u tournament (∀ v tournament (∀ w ℕ
  (= (item (rounds u v (+ w 1)) (+ w 1))
     (list (item (move u (rounds u v w)) 1) (item (move v (rounds v u w)) 1))))))

; the cost of a game is the sum of the losses of all its rounds
(∀ u tournament (∀ v tournament (∀ w ℕ
  (= (cost u v w) (apply + (sequence z (rounds u v w) (loss (item z 1) (item z 2))))))))

An example, played out in full:


(= (rounds tit-for-tat always-defect 4)
   (list (list cooperate defect) (list defect defect) (list defect defect) (list defect defect)))
(= (cost tit-for-tat always-defect 4) 22)

Chance in the game

Generous-tit-for-tat picks its move by chance after a defection, so what a game costs it is not fixed. Its expected loss for a round is the mean over the list it picks from:


; after a defection, against always-defect: 17/3 per round, where
; always defecting back would cost 5
(= (mean (sequence u (list cooperate defect defect) (loss u defect))) (÷ 17 3))
; against itself, it never needs to forgive: it always cooperates
(= (move generous-tit-for-tat (list (list cooperate cooperate))) (list cooperate))

Forgiving costs a little against a player who always defects, but it lets two forgiving players recover from a mistake.

Random pays no attention to the game at all: each move is cooperate or defect with a chance of one half. Against such a player, defecting costs less whatever happens, because nothing one does changes what it does next. Cooperation only pays with a player who answers.


; random cooperates with a chance of one half, at the start and
; after any history
(= (chance cooperate (move random (list))) (÷ 1 2))
(= (chance cooperate (move random (list (list defect defect)))) (÷ 1 2))

; against random, cooperating costs 3 or 7: a mean of 5 per round
(= (mean (sequence u (move random (list)) (loss cooperate u))) 5)
; against random, defecting costs 2 or 5: a mean of 7/2 per round
(= (mean (sequence u (move random (list)) (loss defect u))) (÷ 7 2))

Results over 255 rounds

A game lasts 255 rounds: the largest number a chunk can hold, and so the longest game the message can name with a single number. It stands for the long run. Nothing depends on the exact number, except that it is long enough for the first rounds not to decide the result.


; two that cooperate lose 765 each; two that defect lose 1275 each
(= (cost tit-for-tat tit-for-tat 255) (big 2 253))
(= (cost always-defect always-defect 255) (big 4 251))

; tit-for-tat is exploited only in the first round by always-defect:
; 1277 against 1272
(= (cost tit-for-tat always-defect 255) (big 4 253))
(= (cost always-defect tit-for-tat 255) (big 4 248))

; always-cooperate is exploited in every round: 1785 against 510
(= (cost always-cooperate always-defect 255) (big 6 249))
(= (cost always-defect always-cooperate 255) (big 1 254))

; pavlov is exploited every second round: 1531 against 891
(= (cost pavlov always-defect 255) (big 5 251))
(= (cost always-defect pavlov 255) (big 3 123))

; tit-for-tat against its suspicious twin: the first defection
; echoes back and forth, 1150 and 1145 instead of 765 each
(= (cost tit-for-tat suspicious-tit-for-tat 255) (big 4 126))
(= (cost suspicious-tit-for-tat tit-for-tat 255) (big 4 121))

; grim never forgives that first defection: 1274 against 1274
(= (cost grim suspicious-tit-for-tat 255) (big 4 250))
(= (cost suspicious-tit-for-tat grim 255) (big 4 250))

A tournament: each strategy plays all six, itself included. Always-defect never loses more than the player it meets, yet it ends up second to last. Last of all is the strategy that follows the same rule as tit-for-tat but opens with a defection. Tit-for-tat, which opens in peace, loses least:


; the total loss of each strategy over its six games: 5487, 5611,
; 5614, 5781, 6495, 6923
(= (apply + (sequence u tournament (cost tit-for-tat u 255))) (big 21 111))
(= (apply + (sequence u tournament (cost grim u 255))) (big 21 235))
(= (apply + (sequence u tournament (cost always-cooperate u 255))) (big 21 238))
(= (apply + (sequence u tournament (cost pavlov u 255))) (big 22 149))
(= (apply + (sequence u tournament (cost always-defect u 255))) (big 25 95))
(= (apply + (sequence u tournament (cost suspicious-tit-for-tat u 255))) (big 27 11))
