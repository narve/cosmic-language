humans

The senders of this message are humans: living things of the Earth, each a party that can send and receive messages. Who we are is stated: only we can say it; what has been measured about us, further down, is observed.


; every human is a party
(stated (∀ u humans (∃ v parties (= v u))))
; we, the senders, are humans
(stated (∃ u humans (= u we)))
; and humans live on the Earth
(stated (∀ u humans (at u earth)))

How big we are, in the units of the message. Each value is an average over all humans. The message cannot say "grown-up", so the ranges are wide enough to hold with children counted too; the values in the comments are those of grown-ups.


; our mass: between 2⁹⁴ and 2⁹⁶ proton masses, about 2⁹⁵
(observed (< (^ 2 94) (÷ (average u humans (mass u)) (mass proton)) (^ 2 96)))

; our height is a length
(∀ u humans (= (dim (height u)) L))
; between 2¹¹⁶ and 2¹¹⁷ Planck lengths, about 1.24 × 2¹¹⁶
(observed (< (^ 2 116) (÷ (average u humans (height u)) planck-length) (^ 2 117)))
; and no human is as tall as 2¹¹⁸ Planck lengths
(observed (∀ u humans (< (height u) (× (^ 2 118) planck-length))))

We are warm: warmer than water's triple point, so that the water inside us stays liquid, and not much warmer.


; our temperature: between 9/8 and 73/64 of water's triple point,
; about 1.135
(observed (< (÷ 9 8) (÷ (average u humans (temperature u)) (triple-point water)) (÷ 73 64)))
; every living human is warmer than the triple point of water
(observed (∀ u humans (> (temperature u) (triple-point water))))

What we are made of. Most of our atoms are hydrogen, then oxygen, carbon and nitrogen, with smaller amounts of eight more elements. Water is the most common molecule in us by far, and more than half of our mass. All the molecules of the lesson on the chemistry of life are in us too.


; in every human: more hydrogen atoms than oxygen, more oxygen than
; carbon, more carbon than nitrogen
(observed (∀ u humans
  (> (count hydrogen u) (count oxygen u) (count carbon u) (count nitrogen u))))
; more water molecules than of each of these eleven molecules
(observed (∀ u humans
  (∀ v (list dioxygen glucose glycine adenine guanine atp urea dopamine serotonin adrenaline heme)
    (> (count water u) (count v u)))))
; every human also holds calcium (in the bones), phosphorus,
; potassium, sulfur, sodium, chlorine, magnesium and iron
(observed (∀ u humans
  (∀ v (list calcium phosphorus potassium sulfur sodium chlorine magnesium iron)
    (> (count v u) 0))))
; and more than half of a human's mass is water, on average
(observed (> (average u humans (÷ (× (count water u) (mass water)) (mass u))) (÷ 1 2)))

Our heredity is DNA. genome gives one full set of the DNA molecules in a cell; a cell of ours holds two such sets, one from each parent. A human set is 23 molecules, together a little over three thousand million base pairs.


; a set of a human's DNA: 23 molecules
(observed (∀ u humans (= (length (genome u)) 23)))
; each of them is DNA
(observed (∀ u humans (∀ v (genome u) (∃ w dna (= w v)))))
; together between 2³¹ and 2³² base pairs, about 1.4 × 2³¹
(observed (< (^ 2 31)
              (average u humans (apply + (sequence v (genome u) (length (item (strands v) 1)))))
              (^ 2 32)))
