the Sun (decoy)

This lesson is not in the message. It stands in for the lesson on the Sun in the decoy version only, which is made for fun: to see whether a blind reader places the sender at another star. Here sun is Kepler-1647 A, a star larger and hotter than the Sun, 1.2 kiloparsecs away (about 4000 light-years). It does not shine alone: a second star, almost a twin of the Sun, goes round it every 11 days. earth is the giant planet Kepler-1647 b, which goes round both stars in their habitable zone, and the sender lives on a moon of it. The system is so far from the Sun that the lessons on the Galaxy and on pulsars are replaced too: seen from there, the pulsars lie in other directions, and some of them at another age. Why this system was chosen, and how the numbers were found, is on the decoy page.


; our star is a star, and our planet is a body
(∃ u stars (= u sun))
(∃ u bodies (= u earth))

; our star's mass: between 7 × 2¹⁸⁷ and 2¹⁹⁰ proton masses
(observed (< (× 7 (^ 2 187)) (÷ (mass sun) (mass proton)) (^ 2 190)))
; its radius: between 2¹⁴⁵ and 2¹⁴⁶ Planck lengths
(observed (< (^ 2 145) (÷ (radius sun) planck-length) (^ 2 146)))
; its temperature: between 20 and 25 times the triple point of water
(observed (< 20 (÷ (temperature sun) (triple-point water)) 25))
; its luminosity: between 2⁻⁸⁵ and 2⁻⁸³ of the Planck unit of
; power, c⁵ ÷ G
(observed (< (÷ 1 (^ 2 85)) (÷ (luminosity sun) (÷ (^ c 5) G)) (÷ 1 (^ 2 83))))

A second star goes round ours, close by, and no other star does. It has no name in the message: each sentence about it says "the star that goes round ours". Its mass and temperature are a little smaller than those of our star, and it is about half as wide.


; one other star goes round ours, and only one
(observed (∃ u stars (and (not (= u sun)) (orbits u sun))))
(observed (∀ u stars (∀ v stars (implies (and (orbits u sun) (orbits v sun)) (= u v)))))

; its mass: between 2¹⁸⁹ and 2¹⁹⁰ proton masses
(observed (∃ u stars (and (orbits u sun) (< (^ 2 189) (÷ (mass u) (mass proton)) (^ 2 190)))))
; its radius: between 2¹⁴⁴ and 2¹⁴⁵ Planck lengths
(observed (∃ u stars (and (orbits u sun) (< (^ 2 144) (÷ (radius u) planck-length) (^ 2 145)))))
; its temperature: between 18 and 24 times the triple point of water
(observed (∃ u stars (and (orbits u sun) (< 18 (÷ (temperature u) (triple-point water)) 24))))

; the size of its orbit: between 2¹⁴⁹ and 2¹⁵⁰ Planck lengths
(observed (∃ u stars (and (orbits u sun) (< (^ 2 149) (÷ (axis u sun) planck-length) (^ 2 150)))))
; one round takes between 2¹⁶³ and 2¹⁶⁴ Planck times
(observed (∃ u stars (and (orbits u sun) (< (^ 2 163) (÷ (period u sun) planck-time) (^ 2 164)))))

Our planet goes round both stars, far outside their own orbit. So the rule for orbits holds for it as if the two stars were one star as heavy as both: the mass in the rule is that of all three bodies together. Its orbit, size and mass are given in units that any reader has: Planck lengths, Planck times and proton masses.


; our planet goes round both stars: period² = 4π² × axis³
; ÷ (G × the sum of the masses of the two stars and the planet)
(observed (∃ u stars (and (orbits u sun)
  (= (^ (period earth sun) 2)
     (÷ (× 4 (^ π 2) (^ (axis earth sun) 3))
        (× G (+ (mass sun) (mass u) (mass earth))))))))

; the size of its orbit: between 2¹⁵⁴ and 2¹⁵⁵ Planck lengths
(observed (< (^ 2 154) (÷ (axis earth sun) planck-length) (^ 2 155)))
; one round takes between 2¹⁷⁰ and 2¹⁷¹ Planck times
(observed (< (^ 2 170) (÷ (period earth sun) planck-time) (^ 2 171)))
; its radius: between 2¹⁴¹ and 2¹⁴² Planck lengths
(observed (< (^ 2 141) (÷ (radius earth) planck-length) (^ 2 142)))
; its mass is less than 2¹⁸² proton masses: it is known only
; roughly, from how it pulls the two stars
(observed (< (÷ (mass earth) (mass proton)) (^ 2 182)))

This is where we are. New: at, a party living on a body. Like everything we say about ourselves, it is stated: we live on a body that goes round our planet, not on the planet, which has no solid ground, and not on a star.


; we live on a body that goes round our planet
(stated (∃ u bodies (and (at we u) (orbits u earth))))
; not on the planet itself, and not on our star
(stated (not (at we earth)))
(stated (not (at we sun)))
