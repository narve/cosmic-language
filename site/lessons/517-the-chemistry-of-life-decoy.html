The molecules of the lesson on common molecules are found all over the Galaxy. The ones here matter to us in particular: the air of our planet, and the molecules that life on it, and our own bodies, are built from and run on. A hereditary molecule is two long strands, each a chain of bases held on a backbone of sugar and phosphate. strands gives them as two lists of bases. Each strand has a direction of its own, set by how its backbone is built, and each list is read in that direction. Across the two strands, each base sits opposite its partner: diaminopurine opposite hydroxymethyluracil, xanthine opposite diaminopyrimidine, each pair held by three hydrogen bonds.
