eval

New: eval, the value of a quoted expression, and the domain expressions: numbers, symbols, and lists of expressions. First examples, then the rules that give the value of every expression without variables.


(= (eval (quote (+ 2 3))) 5)
(= (eval (quote (× 2 (+ 1 2)))) 6)
(= (eval 7) 7)

(= (eval (quote (list 1 (+ 1 1)))) (list 1 2))
(= (eval (quote (rest (list 4 7 9)))) (list 7 9))
(= (eval (quote (if (< 1 2) 5 7))) 5)
(= (eval (quote true)) true)
(= (eval (quote π)) π)

; the value of a sentence is true or false; a false sentence can now
; be evaluated without being claimed
(= (eval (quote (= 2 2))) true)
(= (eval (quote (= 2 3))) false)
(= (eval (quote (< 3 2))) false)
(= (eval (quote (and (= 2 2) (= 2 3)))) false)

; the value of a quoted expression is the expression
(= (eval (quote (quote (+ 2 3)))) (quote (+ 2 3)))

; eval can be applied to itself
(= (eval (quote (eval (quote (+ 1 2))))) 3)
; and the value of a let is the value of its body
(= (eval (quote (let u 2 (× u u)))) 4)

Expressions

The rules below speak of all expressions: everything a quoted sentence can be made of.


; numbers, symbols and quoted sentences are expressions
(∃ u expressions (= u 5))
(∃ u expressions (= u (quote +)))
(∃ u expressions (= u (quote (+ 2 3))))
; a list of expressions is an expression
(∀ u expressions (∀ v expressions (∃ w expressions (= w (list u v)))))

The rules

A number is its own value, and so is an operator: it is passed on as it is, the way apply takes +.


(∀ u expressions (implies (= (type u) number) (= (eval u) u)))
(∀ u expressions (implies (= (type u) operator) (= (eval u) u)))

An operation: apply the operator to the values of its arguments. Seven operators work differently and are left out here: quote and if, which do not compute all their arguments, and ∀, ∃, sequence, average and let, which bind a variable.


(∀ u expressions (implies (= (type u) brackets)
  (implies (> (length u) 0)
  (implies (= (type (item u 1)) operator)
  (implies (= (count (item u 1) (quote (quote if ∀ ∃ sequence average let))) 0)
    (= (eval u) (apply (item u 1) (sequence v (rest u) (eval v)))))))))

Quote gives its expression without computing it, and if computes only the branch it picks:


(∀ u expressions (= (eval (list (quote quote) u)) u))
(∀ u expressions (∀ v expressions (∀ w expressions
  (= (eval (list (quote if) u v w)) (if (eval u) (eval v) (eval w))))))

A constant, a particle, an element or another named thing has the thing it names as its value, as in (= (eval (quote π)) π) above. With these few sentences, every expression without variables and without a binder has a value. The binders (∀, ∃, sequence, average and let) get no rules here: their value is what the earlier lessons taught them to mean.
