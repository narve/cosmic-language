three particles

New: the particles electron, proton and neutron (a new type), the functions mass and charge, and the marker observed. Only ratios of masses are given, never a mass on its own, because a mass on its own needs a unit.

the three masses in order: the electron is the lightest, the neutron the heaviest
(observed (< (mass electron) (mass proton) (mass neutron)))

proton ÷ electron: between 1836 and 1837
(1836 is written (big 7 44), because 7 × 256 + 44 = 1836)
(observed (< (big 7 44) (÷ (mass proton) (mass electron)) (big 7 45)))
neutron ÷ electron: between 1838 and 1839
(observed (< (big 7 46) (÷ (mass neutron) (mass electron)) (big 7 47)))
neutron ÷ proton: just above 1, so the neutron is slightly heavier
(observed (< 1 (÷ (mass neutron) (mass proton)) (÷ 101 100)))

the proton and the electron have opposite charges
(observed (= (charge proton) (neg (charge electron))))
the electron's charge is not zero; the neutron's is
(observed (≠ (charge electron) 0))  (observed (= (charge neutron) 0))

Three particles, two of them about 1836 and 1839 times heavier than the third, with charges that are opposite and zero. A receiver who knows physics will recognize them.
