stars

The message has bodies, gravity, light, temperature and the elements. Together they describe a star. New: the domain stars, and the functions radius and luminosity, the power a star sends out as light.

; every star is a body: it has a mass, and gravity acts on it
(∀ u stars (∃ v bodies (= u v)))
(∀ u stars (> (mass u) 0))

; a star has a size, a temperature and a luminosity: a length, a
; temperature and a power (energy per time)
(∀ u stars (= (dim (radius u)) L))
(∀ u stars (= (dim (temperature u)) Θ))
(∀ u stars (= (dim (luminosity u)) (÷ (× M (^ L 2)) (^ T 3))))

; the light leaves through its surface: luminosity = 4π × radius² ×
; radiance (the power per area)
(∀ u stars (= (luminosity u) (× 4 π (^ (radius u) 2) (radiance u))))
; the temperature of a star is that of a black body with the same
; radiance
(∀ u stars (= (radiance u)
              (× (÷ (^ π 2) 60) (÷ (^ kB 4) (× (^ ħ 3) (^ c 2))) (^ (temperature u) 4))))
; so its luminosity follows from its radius and its temperature
(∀ u stars (= (luminosity u)
              (× 4 π (^ (radius u) 2)
                 (× (÷ (^ π 2) 60) (÷ (^ kB 4) (× (^ ħ 3) (^ c 2))) (^ (temperature u) 4)))))

Stars are made mostly of hydrogen, then helium. They shine by turning hydrogen into helium: four hydrogen atoms weigh more than one atom of helium with two neutrons, and the mass that is missing leaves the star as energy, mass × c².

; in a star there are more hydrogen atoms than helium atoms, and
; more helium atoms than carbon or oxygen atoms
(observed (∀ u stars (> (count hydrogen u) (count helium u))))
(observed (∀ u stars (> (count helium u) (count carbon u))))
(observed (∀ u stars (> (count helium u) (count oxygen u))))

; one helium atom weighs less than four hydrogen atoms: between 1 −
; 1/2⁷ and 1 − 1/2⁸ of them
; (about 0.7 % of the mass goes)
(observed (< (− 1 (÷ 1 (^ 2 7)))
              (÷ (mass (isotope helium 2)) (× 4 (mass (isotope hydrogen 0))))
              (− 1 (÷ 1 (^ 2 8)))))

New: orbits, one body going round another under gravity, period, the time one round takes, and axis, the size of the orbit: half the sum of the smallest and the largest distance. Newton's law of gravitation gives the rule that relates them.

; a period is a time, an axis is a length
(∀ u bodies (∀ v bodies (implies (orbits u v) (= (dim (period u v)) T))))
(∀ u bodies (∀ v bodies (implies (orbits u v) (= (dim (axis u v)) L))))
; an orbit has a size, and one round takes time
(∀ u bodies (∀ v bodies (implies (orbits u v) (and (> (axis u v) 0) (> (period u v) 0)))))

; the rule: period² = 4π² × axis³ ÷ (G × the sum of the two masses)
(observed (∀ u bodies (∀ v bodies
  (implies (orbits u v)
    (= (^ (period u v) 2)
       (÷ (× 4 (^ π 2) (^ (axis u v) 3))
          (× G (+ (mass u) (mass v)))))))))

Sources: AME2020 (masses of hydrogen and helium). Details in the specification, §8.4.
