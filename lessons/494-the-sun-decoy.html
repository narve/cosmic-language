the Sun (decoy)

This lesson is not in the message. It stands in for the lesson on the Sun in the decoy version only, which is made for fun: to see whether a blind reader places the sender at another star. Here sun and earth are Proxima Centauri, the nearest star, and its planet Proxima b. It is near enough that the lessons on the Galaxy and on pulsars still hold as they are: seen from it, no pulsar's direction changes by more than about half a degree.

; our star is a star, and our planet is a body
(∃ u stars (= u sun))
(∃ u bodies (= u earth))

; our star's mass: between 2¹⁸⁶ and 2¹⁸⁷ proton masses
(observed (< (^ 2 186) (÷ (mass sun) (mass proton)) (^ 2 187)))
; its radius: between 35 × 2¹³⁵ and 244 × 2¹³⁵ Planck lengths
(observed (< (× 35 (^ 2 135)) (÷ (radius sun) planck-length) (× 244 (^ 2 135))))
; its temperature: between 2³ and 2⁴ times the triple point of water
(observed (< (^ 2 3) (÷ (temperature sun) (triple-point water)) (^ 2 4)))
; its luminosity: between 117/2¹⁰³ and 206/2¹⁰³ of the Planck unit
; of power, c⁵ ÷ G
(observed (< (÷ 117 (^ 2 103)) (÷ (luminosity sun) (÷ (^ c 5) G)) (÷ 206 (^ 2 103))))

Our planet goes round our star. Its orbit and its mass are given in units that any reader has: Planck lengths, Planck times and proton masses.

; our planet orbits our star
(observed (orbits earth sun))
; the size of its orbit: between 2¹⁴⁸ and 2¹⁴⁹ Planck lengths
(observed (< (^ 2 148) (÷ (axis earth sun) planck-length) (^ 2 149)))
; one round takes between 2¹⁶³ and 2¹⁶⁴ Planck times
(observed (< (^ 2 163) (÷ (period earth sun) planck-time) (^ 2 164)))
; its mass is more than 2¹⁷⁰ proton masses: only a lower bound
; is known, as the true mass depends on the tilt of its orbit
(observed (< (^ 2 170) (÷ (mass earth) (mass proton))))

This is where we are. New: at, a party living on a body. Like everything we say about ourselves, it is stated: this star is our star.

; we live on the planet, which goes round the star
(stated (at we earth))
(stated (∃ u bodies (and (at we u) (orbits u sun))))
; we do not live on the star itself
(stated (not (at we sun)))

Sources: NASA Exoplanet Archive, composite parameters of Proxima Cen b, from A. Suárez Mascareño and others (2025); NASA Sun and Earth fact sheets (the units of the archive); CODATA 2022 (the Planck units and the proton mass). Details in the specification, §8.4.
