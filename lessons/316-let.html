let

A long expression can be given a short name inside a sentence. New: let. (let u x S) says S, with the variable u standing for the value x everywhere in S. Like ∀, it binds its variable; unlike ∀, it gives it a single value.

; u is 0, and u is 0
(let u 0 (= u 0))
; u is 4, and u + u is 8
(let u 4 (= (+ u u) 8))

; the value can be an expression: u is 2 + 3, and u × u is 25
(let u (+ 2 3) (= (× u u) 25))

; or a list
(let u (list 1 2 3) (= (length u) 3))
(let u (list 1 2 3) (= (item u (length u)) 3))

A let can hold another, and a later value may use an earlier name. It says the same as ∀ over a list of one item.

; one let inside another
(let u 2 (let v 3 (= (+ u v) 5)))
; the second value uses the first name
(let u 2 (let v (× u u) (= (+ u v) 6)))

; the same as ∀ over a list of one item
(= (let u 7 (> u 5)) (∀ u (list 7) (> u 5)))
; and the same when both are false: 3 is not greater than 5
(= (let u 3 (> u 5)) (∀ u (list 3) (> u 5)))

; and it works inside laws: for every u, u + 1 is greater than u
(∀ u ℕ (let v (+ u 1) (> v u)))
