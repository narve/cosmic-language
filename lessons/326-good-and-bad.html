good and bad

A party can judge a conversation. (good u z) means that u finds the conversation z good, and (bad u z) that u finds it bad. A conversation is written as a list of messages, and a message as a list of sentences: a question in the first message, a reply in the second. The conversations judged here are examples, not ones that took place. Good and bad are always someone's judgement, and the judgements here are ours.


; to "what is 2 + 3?", we find the reply 5 good
(let w (quote (= (+ 2 3) ?))
  (let v (fill w 5)
    (good we (list (list w) (list v)))))
; and the reply 4 bad: it is wrong
(let w (quote (= (+ 2 3) ?))
  (let v (fill w 4)
    (bad we (list (list w) (list v)))))

; to "is 2 less than 3?", we find yes good
(let w (quote (= (< 2 3) ?))
  (let v (fill w true)
    (good we (list (list w) (list v)))))
; and no bad
(let w (quote (= (< 2 3) ?))
  (let v (fill w false)
    (bad we (list (list w) (list v)))))

Good is not the same as true. A right reply can be bad, because it does not do what the question asked. This is the step that Hans Freudenthal's Lincos (1960) took in the same way: there a speaker calls the reply 10/4 to a question bad, although it is right, because it is not written in lowest terms.


; a reply can be right and still bad
(let w (quote (= (+ 2 3) ?))
  (∃ v expressions
    (and (= (eval (fill w v)) true)
         (bad we (list (list w) (list (fill w v)))))))
; these replies are right, but we find them bad: they do not say
; which number
(let w (quote (= (+ 2 3) ?))
  (∀ v (list (quote (+ 2 3)) (quote (+ 1 4)) (quote (÷ 15 3))
             (quote (length (list 1 2 3 4 5))))
    (bad we (list (list w) (list (fill w v))))))

; to "which natural numbers are greater than 1 and less than 5?",
; we find the reply 2, 3, 4 good
(let w (quote (∀ u ? (and (∃ v ℕ (= v u)) (< 1 u 5))))
  (let v (fill w (quote (list 2 3 4)))
    (good we (list (list w) (list v)))))
; and the reply 2, 3 bad: it is right, but it leaves one out
(let w (quote (∀ u ? (and (∃ v ℕ (= v u)) (< 1 u 5))))
  (let v (fill w (quote (list 2 3)))
    (bad we (list (list w) (list v)))))

; the same reply can be good after one question and bad after
; another: 1 + 1 = 2 is good after "what is 1 + 1?"
(let w (quote (= (+ 1 1) ?))
  (let v (quote (= (+ 1 1) 2))
    (good we (list (list w) (list v)))))
; and bad after "what is 2 + 3?", which it does not answer
(let w (quote (= (+ 2 3) ?))
  (let v (quote (= (+ 1 1) 2))
    (bad we (list (list w) (list v)))))

; nothing is both good and bad to the same party
(∀ u parties (∀ v expressions (not (and (good u v) (bad u v)))))
; but good and bad are not the same for everyone: there are parties
; u and v and a conversation w that u finds good and v finds bad
(∃ u parties (∃ v parties (∃ w expressions (and (good u w) (bad v w)))))

; and one thing we find bad whenever we meet it: a message that
; says something false
(∀ u expressions (implies (= (eval u) false) (bad we (list (list u)))))
