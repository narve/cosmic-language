the prisoner's dilemma

New: the moves cooperate and defect, and payoff, the score for a move given the other player's move.

(= (payoff cooperate cooperate) 3)  (= (payoff cooperate defect) 0)
(= (payoff defect cooperate) 5)  (= (payoff defect defect) 1)

; defecting always pays more, whatever the other does …
(> (payoff defect cooperate) (payoff cooperate cooperate) (payoff defect defect) (payoff cooperate defect))
; … yet cooperating together beats taking turns to exploit each other
(> (× 2 (payoff cooperate cooperate)) (+ (payoff defect cooperate) (payoff cooperate defect)))

Strategies

New: the domain histories, the function move, and eight strategies. A history is a list of the rounds played so far, each round a list of two moves: one's own, then the other's. (move s h) is a list to pick the next move from: a single move for a fixed choice, repeated moves for a chance.

; always-cooperate and always-defect ignore the history
(∀ u histories (= (move always-cooperate u) (list cooperate)))
(∀ u histories (= (move always-defect u) (list defect)))

; tit-for-tat: cooperate first, then do what the other did last
(= (move tit-for-tat (list)) (list cooperate))
(∀ u histories (implies (> (length u) 0)
  (= (move tit-for-tat u) (list (item (item u (length u)) 2)))))

; suspicious-tit-for-tat: the same rule, but it defects first
(= (move suspicious-tit-for-tat (list)) (list defect))
(∀ u histories (implies (> (length u) 0)
  (= (move suspicious-tit-for-tat u) (list (item (item u (length u)) 2)))))

; grim: cooperate as long as the other has never defected
(∀ u histories (implies (= (count defect (sequence v u (item v 2))) 0)
  (= (move grim u) (list cooperate))))
(∀ u histories (implies (> (count defect (sequence v u (item v 2))) 0)
  (= (move grim u) (list defect))))

; pavlov: cooperate if both did the same last time, otherwise defect
(= (move pavlov (list)) (list cooperate))
(∀ u histories (implies (and (> (length u) 0)
                                (= (item (item u (length u)) 1) (item (item u (length u)) 2)))
  (= (move pavlov u) (list cooperate))))
(∀ u histories (implies (and (> (length u) 0)
                                (≠ (item (item u (length u)) 1) (item (item u (length u)) 2)))
  (= (move pavlov u) (list defect))))

; generous-tit-for-tat: like tit-for-tat, but after a defection it forgives once in three
(= (move generous-tit-for-tat (list)) (list cooperate))
(∀ u histories (implies (and (> (length u) 0) (= (item (item u (length u)) 2) cooperate))
  (= (move generous-tit-for-tat u) (list cooperate))))
(∀ u histories (implies (and (> (length u) 0) (= (item (item u (length u)) 2) defect))
  (= (move generous-tit-for-tat u) (list cooperate defect defect))))

; random: whatever has happened, it picks one of the two moves by chance
(∀ u histories (= (move random u) (list cooperate defect)))

The same strategies with if

implies states that something holds where a condition holds; if picks a value. Two sentences with opposite conditions, like those above, say the same as one sentence with if. So the strategies are defined twice, on purpose: each form helps the receiver read the other.

; tit-for-tat: cooperate first, then do what the other did last
(∀ u histories (= (move tit-for-tat u)
  (if (= (length u) 0) (list cooperate) (list (item (item u (length u)) 2)))))

; suspicious-tit-for-tat: defect first, then do what the other did last
(∀ u histories (= (move suspicious-tit-for-tat u)
  (if (= (length u) 0) (list defect) (list (item (item u (length u)) 2)))))

; grim: cooperate as long as the other has never defected
(∀ u histories (= (move grim u)
  (if (= (count defect (sequence v u (item v 2))) 0) (list cooperate) (list defect))))

; pavlov: cooperate first, then cooperate if both did the same last time
(∀ u histories (= (move pavlov u)
  (if (= (length u) 0)
      (list cooperate)
      (if (= (item (item u (length u)) 1) (item (item u (length u)) 2)) (list cooperate) (list defect)))))

Playing a game

New: tournament, the list of the six strategies that always make a fixed choice, and the functions rounds (the history after a number of rounds) and score. First the rules: each round, each player takes its strategy's move given the rounds so far, seen from its own side, which is the same game with the players swapped.

(= tournament (list always-cooperate always-defect tit-for-tat suspicious-tit-for-tat grim pavlov))
(∀ u tournament (∀ v tournament (= (rounds u v 0) (list))))
(∀ u tournament (∀ v tournament (∀ w ℕ (= (length (rounds u v w)) w))))

; earlier rounds stay as they were
(∀ u tournament (∀ v tournament (∀ w ℕ (∀ z ℕ (implies (≤ 1 z w)
  (= (item (rounds u v w) z) (item (rounds u v z) z)))))))

; the same game seen from the other side: each round with its two moves swapped
(∀ u tournament (∀ v tournament (∀ w ℕ (∀ z ℕ (implies (≤ 1 z w)
  (= (item (item (rounds u v w) z) 1) (item (item (rounds v u w) z) 2)))))))

; the next round: each player's move, given the game so far from its own side
(∀ u tournament (∀ v tournament (∀ w ℕ
  (= (item (rounds u v (+ w 1)) (+ w 1))
     (list (item (move u (rounds u v w)) 1) (item (move v (rounds v u w)) 1))))))

; the score is the sum of the payoffs of all rounds
(∀ u tournament (∀ v tournament (∀ w ℕ
  (= (score u v w) (apply + (sequence z (rounds u v w) (payoff (item z 1) (item z 2))))))))

An example, played out in full:

(= (rounds tit-for-tat always-defect 4)
   (list (list cooperate defect) (list defect defect) (list defect defect) (list defect defect)))
(= (score tit-for-tat always-defect 4) 3)

Chance in the game

Generous-tit-for-tat picks its move by chance after a defection, so its score is not fixed. Its expected payoff for a round is the mean over the list it picks from:

; after a defection, against always-defect: two thirds per round
(= (mean (sequence u (list cooperate defect defect) (payoff u defect))) (÷ 2 3))
; against itself, it never needs to forgive: it always cooperates
(= (move generous-tit-for-tat (list (list cooperate cooperate))) (list cooperate))

Forgiving costs a little against a player who always defects, but it lets two forgiving players recover from a mistake.

Random pays no attention to the game at all: each move is cooperate or defect with a chance of one half. Against such a player, defecting pays more whatever happens, because nothing one does changes what it does next. Cooperation only pays with a player who answers.

; random cooperates with a chance of one half, at the start and after any history
(= (chance cooperate (move random (list))) (÷ 1 2))
(= (chance cooperate (move random (list (list defect defect)))) (÷ 1 2))

; against random, cooperating gets 3 or 0: a mean of 3/2 per round
(= (mean (sequence u (move random (list)) (payoff cooperate u))) (÷ 3 2))
; against random, defecting gets 5 or 1: a mean of 3 per round
(= (mean (sequence u (move random (list)) (payoff defect u))) 3)

Results over 251 rounds

A game lasts 251 rounds: the largest prime number that fits in one chunk. Nothing depends on that number, except that it is long enough for the first rounds not to decide the result.

; two that cooperate get 753 each: (big 2 241); two that defect get 251 each
(= (score tit-for-tat tit-for-tat 251) (big 2 241))  (= (score always-defect always-defect 251) 251)

; tit-for-tat loses only the first round to always-defect: 250 against 255
(= (score tit-for-tat always-defect 251) 250)  (= (score always-defect tit-for-tat 251) 255)

; always-cooperate is exploited in every round: 0 against 1255
(= (score always-cooperate always-defect 251) 0)  (= (score always-defect always-cooperate 251) (big 4 231))

; pavlov is exploited every second round: 125 against 755
(= (score pavlov always-defect 251) 125)  (= (score always-defect pavlov 251) (big 2 243))

; tit-for-tat against its suspicious twin: the first defection echoes back and forth, 625 and 630 instead of 753 each
(= (score tit-for-tat suspicious-tit-for-tat 251) (big 2 113))  (= (score suspicious-tit-for-tat tit-for-tat 251) (big 2 118))

; grim never forgives that first defection: 254 against 254
(= (score grim suspicious-tit-for-tat 251) 254)  (= (score suspicious-tit-for-tat grim 251) 254)

A tournament: each strategy plays all six, itself included. Always-defect never loses a single game, yet it ends up second to last. Last of all is the strategy that follows the same rule as tit-for-tat but opens with a defection. Tit-for-tat, which opens in peace, is first:

; the total of each strategy over its six games: 3887, 3762, 3640, 3516, 3022, 2644
(= (apply + (sequence u tournament (score tit-for-tat u 251))) (big 15 47))
(= (apply + (sequence u tournament (score always-cooperate u 251))) (big 14 178))
(= (apply + (sequence u tournament (score pavlov u 251))) (big 14 56))
(= (apply + (sequence u tournament (score grim u 251))) (big 13 188))
(= (apply + (sequence u tournament (score always-defect u 251))) (big 11 206))
(= (apply + (sequence u tournament (score suspicious-tit-for-tat u 251))) (big 10 84))

Peace wins

The message ends with what the tournament shows. Two players who keep the peace each get three times what two players at war get. The two strategies that defect first end with the lowest totals, and the four that never defect first all end above them. And tit-for-tat, which opens in peace, answers in kind and forgives as soon as the other returns to peace, ends with more than every other strategy. That is the last statement of the message.

; two at peace get more, each, than two at war: 753 against 251
(> (score tit-for-tat tit-for-tat 251) (score always-defect always-defect 251))

; the whole ranking, from last to first: the two that defect first are at the bottom
(< (apply + (sequence v tournament (score suspicious-tit-for-tat v 251)))
   (apply + (sequence v tournament (score always-defect v 251)))
   (apply + (sequence v tournament (score grim v 251)))
   (apply + (sequence v tournament (score pavlov v 251)))
   (apply + (sequence v tournament (score always-cooperate v 251)))
   (apply + (sequence v tournament (score tit-for-tat v 251))))

; tit-for-tat ends with more than every other strategy
(∀ u tournament
  (implies (≠ u tit-for-tat)
    (> (apply + (sequence v tournament (score tit-for-tat v 251)))
       (apply + (sequence v tournament (score u v 251))))))
