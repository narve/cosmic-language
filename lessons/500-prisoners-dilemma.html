the prisoner's dilemma

New: the moves cooperate and defect, and payoff, the score for a move given the other player's move.

(= (payoff cooperate cooperate) 3)  (= (payoff cooperate defect) 0)
(= (payoff defect cooperate) 5)  (= (payoff defect defect) 1)
defecting always pays more, whatever the other does …
(> (payoff defect cooperate) (payoff cooperate cooperate) (payoff defect defect) (payoff cooperate defect))
… yet cooperating together beats taking turns to exploit each other
(> (× 2 (payoff cooperate cooperate)) (+ (payoff defect cooperate) (payoff cooperate defect)))

Strategies

New: the domain histories, the function move, and six strategies. A history is a list of the rounds played so far, each round a list of two moves: one's own, then the other's. (move s h) is a list to pick the next move from: a single move for a fixed choice, repeated moves for a chance.

always-cooperate and always-defect ignore the history
(∀ u histories (= (move always-cooperate u) (list cooperate)))
(∀ u histories (= (move always-defect u) (list defect)))
tit-for-tat: cooperate first, then do what the other did last
(= (move tit-for-tat (list)) (list cooperate))
(∀ u histories (implies (> (length u) 0)
  (= (move tit-for-tat u) (list (item (item u (length u)) 2)))))
grim: cooperate as long as the other has never defected
(∀ u histories (implies (= (count defect (sequence v u (item v 2))) 0)
  (= (move grim u) (list cooperate))))
(∀ u histories (implies (> (count defect (sequence v u (item v 2))) 0)
  (= (move grim u) (list defect))))
pavlov: cooperate if both did the same last time, otherwise defect
(= (move pavlov (list)) (list cooperate))
(∀ u histories (implies (and (> (length u) 0)
                                (= (item (item u (length u)) 1) (item (item u (length u)) 2)))
  (= (move pavlov u) (list cooperate))))
(∀ u histories (implies (and (> (length u) 0)
                                (≠ (item (item u (length u)) 1) (item (item u (length u)) 2)))
  (= (move pavlov u) (list defect))))
generous-tit-for-tat: like tit-for-tat, but after a defection it forgives once in three
(= (move generous-tit-for-tat (list)) (list cooperate))
(∀ u histories (implies (and (> (length u) 0) (= (item (item u (length u)) 2) cooperate))
  (= (move generous-tit-for-tat u) (list cooperate))))
(∀ u histories (implies (and (> (length u) 0) (= (item (item u (length u)) 2) defect))
  (= (move generous-tit-for-tat u) (list cooperate defect defect))))

Playing a game

New: tournament, the list of the five strategies that always make a fixed choice, and the functions rounds (the history after a number of rounds) and score. First the rules: each round, each player takes its strategy's move given the rounds so far, seen from its own side, which is the same game with the players swapped.

(= tournament (list always-cooperate always-defect tit-for-tat grim pavlov))
(∀ u tournament (∀ v tournament (= (rounds u v 0) (list))))
(∀ u tournament (∀ v tournament (∀ w ℕ (= (length (rounds u v w)) w))))
earlier rounds stay as they were
(∀ u tournament (∀ v tournament (∀ w ℕ (∀ z ℕ (implies (≤ 1 z w)
  (= (item (rounds u v w) z) (item (rounds u v z) z)))))))
the next round: each player's move, given the game so far from its own side
(∀ u tournament (∀ v tournament (∀ w ℕ
  (= (item (rounds u v (+ w 1)) (+ w 1))
     (list (item (move u (rounds u v w)) 1) (item (move v (rounds v u w)) 1))))))
the score is the sum of the payoffs of all rounds
(∀ u tournament (∀ v tournament (∀ w ℕ
  (= (score u v w) (apply + (sequence z (rounds u v w) (payoff (item z 1) (item z 2))))))))

An example, played out in full:

(= (rounds tit-for-tat always-defect 4)
   (list (list cooperate defect) (list defect defect) (list defect defect) (list defect defect)))
(= (score tit-for-tat always-defect 4) 3)

Results over 10 rounds

(= (score tit-for-tat tit-for-tat 10) 30)  (= (score always-defect always-defect 10) 10)
(= (score tit-for-tat always-defect 10) 9)  (= (score always-defect tit-for-tat 10) 14)
(= (score always-cooperate always-defect 10) 0)  (= (score always-defect always-cooperate 10) 50)
(= (score pavlov always-defect 10) 5)  (= (score always-defect pavlov 10) 30)

A tournament: each strategy plays all five, itself included. Defecting never loses a single game, yet it ends up last. The strategies that cooperate first but cannot be exploited for long come out on top:

(= (apply + (sequence u tournament (score tit-for-tat u 10))) 129)
(= (apply + (sequence u tournament (score grim u 10))) 129)
(= (apply + (sequence u tournament (score pavlov u 10))) 125)
(= (apply + (sequence u tournament (score always-cooperate u 10))) 120)
(= (apply + (sequence u tournament (score always-defect u 10))) 118)

Chance in the game

Generous-tit-for-tat picks its move by chance after a defection, so its score is not fixed. Its expected payoff for a round is the mean over the list it picks from:

after a defection, against always-defect: two thirds per round
(= (mean (sequence u (list cooperate defect defect) (payoff u defect))) (÷ 2 3))
against itself, it never needs to forgive: it always cooperates
(= (move generous-tit-for-tat (list (list cooperate cooperate))) (list cooperate))

Forgiving costs a little against a player who always defects, but it lets two forgiving players recover from a mistake.
