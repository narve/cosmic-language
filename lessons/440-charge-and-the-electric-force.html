charge and the electric force

New: the dimension Q (charge), the function electric (the electric force between two bodies) and the constant ke. The lesson on particles compared charges; now they get a dimension and a law. A positive force pushes apart, a negative one pulls together.

; charge has a dimension of its own, Q, different from the other three
(= (dim (charge proton)) Q)  (= (dim (charge electron)) Q)
(≠ Q M)  (≠ Q L)  (≠ Q T)

; Coulomb's law: electric force = ke × charge × charge ÷ distance²
; (as for gravity, only where the distance is above 0)
(observed (∀ u bodies (∀ v bodies
  (implies (> (distance u v) 0)
    (= (electric u v)
       (÷ (× ke (charge u) (charge v)) (^ (distance u v) 2)))))))

; the dimension of ke, which makes the law come out as a force
(= (dim ke) (÷ (× M (^ L 3)) (× (^ T 2) (^ Q 2))))
; the electric force is a force like gravity: mass × length ÷ time²
(∀ u bodies (∀ v bodies (= (dim (electric u v)) (÷ (× M L) (^ T 2)))))

; opposite charges pull together (a force below 0), equal charges push apart (above 0)
(observed (< (electric proton electron) 0))  (observed (> (electric proton proton) 0))

Because the law is written out, the next number does not depend on how charge is measured. It is the fine-structure constant, which sets the strength of the electric force:

; the fine-structure constant, ke × charge² ÷ (ħ × c): between 1/138 and 1/137
(observed (< (÷ 1 138) (÷ (× ke (^ (charge proton) 2)) (× ħ c)) (÷ 1 137)))

; between a proton and an electron, the electric force is about 2.3 × 10³⁹ times the force of gravity:
; between 2¹³⁰ and 2¹³¹ times (the distance does not matter: it cancels)
(observed (< (^ 2 130)
              (÷ (abs (electric proton electron)) (gravity proton electron))
              (^ 2 131)))

Sources: CODATA 2022 (fine-structure constant, G, masses). Details in the specification, §8.4.
