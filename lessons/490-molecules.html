molecules

New: molecules (a new type), and the functions atoms and bonds. Atoms other than the noble gases join into molecules. count tells how many atoms of an element a molecule contains, and counting goes on through the atoms to their particles:

; water: two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom
(= (count hydrogen water) 2)  (= (count oxygen water) 1)
; counting through the atoms: 1 + 1 + 8 = 10 protons, and as many electrons
(= (count proton water) 10)  (= (count electron water) 10)

; methane: one carbon and four hydrogen
(= (count carbon methane) 1)  (= (count hydrogen methane) 4)
; ammonia: one nitrogen and three hydrogen
(= (count nitrogen ammonia) 1)  (= (count hydrogen ammonia) 3)
; carbon dioxide: one carbon and two oxygen
(= (count carbon carbon-dioxide) 1)  (= (count oxygen carbon-dioxide) 2)

atoms gives a molecule's atoms as a list. bonds gives its bonds: each bond is a list of the positions of two atoms and the number of electron pairs they share.

; water: the oxygen (atom 1) shares one pair with each hydrogen (atoms 2 and 3)
(= (atoms water) (list oxygen hydrogen hydrogen))
(= (bonds water) (list (list 1 2 1) (list 1 3 1)))

; carbon dioxide: the carbon shares two pairs with each oxygen
(= (atoms carbon-dioxide) (list carbon oxygen oxygen))
(= (bonds carbon-dioxide) (list (list 1 2 2) (list 1 3 2)))

; methane: the carbon shares one pair with each of the four hydrogens
(= (atoms methane) (list carbon hydrogen hydrogen hydrogen hydrogen))
(= (bonds methane) (list (list 1 2 1) (list 1 3 1) (list 1 4 1) (list 1 5 1)))

; ammonia: the nitrogen shares one pair with each of the three hydrogens
(= (atoms ammonia) (list nitrogen hydrogen hydrogen hydrogen))
(= (bonds ammonia) (list (list 1 2 1) (list 1 3 1) (list 1 4 1)))

; the count of an element in a molecule is its count in the list of atoms
(= (count hydrogen water) (count hydrogen (atoms water)))

; a molecule is a token of a new type, 9
(= (type (quote water)) 9)

Four molecules that will matter later, given by their atoms:

; adenine: 5 carbon, 5 hydrogen, 5 nitrogen
(= (count carbon adenine) 5)  (= (count hydrogen adenine) 5)  (= (count nitrogen adenine) 5)

; thymine: 5 carbon, 6 hydrogen, 2 nitrogen, 2 oxygen
(= (count carbon thymine) 5)  (= (count hydrogen thymine) 6)  (= (count nitrogen thymine) 2)  (= (count oxygen thymine) 2)

; guanine: 5 carbon, 5 hydrogen, 5 nitrogen, 1 oxygen
(= (count carbon guanine) 5)  (= (count hydrogen guanine) 5)  (= (count nitrogen guanine) 5)  (= (count oxygen guanine) 1)

; cytosine: 4 carbon, 5 hydrogen, 3 nitrogen, 1 oxygen
(= (count carbon cytosine) 4)  (= (count hydrogen cytosine) 5)  (= (count nitrogen cytosine) 3)  (= (count oxygen cytosine) 1)

New: triple-point. Now that water is known, the message can name a temperature that is the same everywhere: water's triple point, where ice, liquid water and vapour exist together. Its thermal energy is compared with hydrogen's step from level 1 to level 2. The range is narrow on purpose, about one part in 300 (roughly a kelvin wide): a wide range would also fit water's boiling, or any other temperature in between.

; kB × the triple point of water, divided by hydrogen's step from level 1 to level 2:
; between 302/2¹⁷ and 303/2¹⁷, which pins the temperature down to one part in 300
; (302 is written (big 1 46), because 256 + 46 = 302)
(observed (< (÷ (big 1 46) (^ 2 17))
              (÷ (× kB (triple-point water)) (− (level hydrogen 2) (level hydrogen 1)))
              (÷ (big 1 47) (^ 2 17))))

; the triple point is a temperature
(= (dim (triple-point water)) Θ)
; in Planck units: between 156/2¹⁰⁶ and 157/2¹⁰⁶ Planck temperatures
(observed (< (÷ 156 (^ 2 106))
              (÷ (triple-point water) planck-temperature)
              (÷ 157 (^ 2 106))))
