subtraction and negative numbers

New symbol: −. To begin with, only differences that stay at or above zero:

taking away 1 gives the number before
(= (− 1 1) 0) (= (− 2 1) 1) (= (− 3 1) 2) (= (− 4 1) 3)
(= (− 5 3) 2) (= (− 9 0) 9) (= (− 7 7) 0) (= (− (big 1 0) 1) 255)
− undoes +
(= (+ (− 5 3) 3) 5)

Then the difference goes below zero, and a new symbol, neg, is needed for the result:

counting down past 0
(= (− 2 1) 1) (= (− 1 1) 0) (= (− 0 1) (neg 1)) (= (− 0 2) (neg 2)) (= (− 0 3) (neg 3))
(= (− 3 5) (neg 2)) (= (− 0 1) (neg 1)) (= (− 2 9) (neg 7))
neg is the opposite of +
(= (+ (neg 2) 2) 0) (= (+ 5 (neg 5)) 0)
(= (neg 0) 0) (= (neg (neg 4)) 4)
(< (neg 3) (neg 1)) (< (neg 1) 0) (> 0 (neg 5))
(= (× (neg 2) 3) (neg 6)) (= (× (neg 2) (neg 3)) 6)

Strictly, − is not needed once neg exists: (− u v) is the same as (+ u (neg v)). It is included anyway, because the message values clarity over a minimal vocabulary. neg is deliberately a separate symbol from −. A symbol never changes meaning with the number of arguments it gets.
