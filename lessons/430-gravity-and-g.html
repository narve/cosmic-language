gravity and G

New: the domain bodies (things with mass), the functions gravity (the force of gravity between two bodies) and distance, and the constant G. Newton's law of gravitation is written out in full. It holds for bodies that move slowly and are not too heavy or close, which is the case the domain bodies stands for. The two variables may stand for the same body, and then the distance is 0 and the law would divide by zero, so the law is stated only where the distance is above 0:

; the distance is the same from either side, and it is a length
(∀ u bodies (∀ v bodies (= (distance u v) (distance v u))))
(∀ u bodies (∀ v bodies (= (dim (distance u v)) L)))

; Newton's law: gravity = G × mass × mass ÷ distance²
; (only where the distance is above 0: a body does not pull on
; itself)
(observed (∀ u bodies (∀ v bodies
  (implies (> (distance u v) 0)
    (= (gravity u v)
       (÷ (× G (mass u) (mass v)) (^ (distance u v) 2)))))))

; the dimension of a force: mass × length ÷ time²
(∀ u bodies (∀ v bodies (= (dim (gravity u v)) (÷ (× M L) (^ T 2)))))
; the dimension of G, which makes the law come out as a force:
; length³ ÷ (mass × time²)
(= (dim G) (÷ (^ L 3) (× M (^ T 2))))

; each body pulls the other with the same force
(observed (∀ u bodies (∀ v bodies (= (gravity u v) (gravity v u)))))

; G × mass² ÷ (ħ × c) is a plain number: it measures the strength of
; gravity between two protons
(= (dim (÷ (× G (^ (mass proton) 2)) (× ħ c))) 1)
