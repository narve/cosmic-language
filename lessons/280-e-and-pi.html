e and π

New constants: e and π. Both are introduced by a sentence that pins them down exactly.

; e is exp of 1
(= e (exp 1))

; it lies between 2 and 3
(< 2 e 3)
; exp of a natural number is a power of e
(∀ u ℕ (= (exp u) (^ e u)))

π is the only number between 0 and 4 for which exp of i times that number is (neg 1):

; the sentence that pins π down: it lies between 0 and 4, and exp of i × π is neg 1
(and (< 0 π 4) (= (exp (× i π)) (neg 1)))

; Euler's identity: exp of i × π, plus 1, is 0
(= (+ (exp (× i π)) 1) 0)
; a full turn gives 1, a quarter turn gives i
(= (exp (× 2 i π)) 1)  (= (exp (× i (÷ π 2))) i)

; π lies between 3 and 4
(< 3 π 4)

Two infinite sums lead to the same π, found in a completely different way. One definition would be enough; three that agree leave little room for a wrong reading.

; π = 4 × (1 − 1/3 + 1/5 − 1/7 + …)
(= π (× 4 (apply + (sequence u ℕ (÷ (^ (neg 1) u) (+ (× 2 u) 1))))))

; 1 + 1/4 + 1/9 + 1/16 + … = π²/6
(= (apply + (sequence u ℕ (÷ 1 (^ (+ u 1) 2)))) (÷ (^ π 2) 6))
