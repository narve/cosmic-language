algebraic numbers

A polynomial is an expression built from a variable, numbers, +, × and ^. The laws of the previous lessons apply to them:

(∀ u ℝ (= (^ (+ u 1) 2) (+ (^ u 2) (× 2 u) 1)))
(∀ u ℝ (= (× (+ u 1) (− u 1)) (− (^ u 2) 1)))

A number that makes a polynomial equal to 0 is a root of it. The square root of 2 from the lesson on real numbers is one; here are more:

a root of u² − u − 1
(∃ u ℝ (and (> u 0) (= (^ u 2) (+ u 1))))
a root of u³ + u + 1
(∃ u ℝ (= (+ (^ u 3) u 1) 0))
a fifth root of 3
(∃ u ℝ (and (> u 0) (= (^ u 5) 3)))

Numbers like these, roots of polynomials with whole-number coefficients, are called algebraic numbers. Every rational number is one: (÷ 1 3) is the root of 3u − 1.
