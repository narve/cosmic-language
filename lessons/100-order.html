order


; each number is less than the next …
(< 0 1)
(< 1 2)
(< 2 3)
(< 3 4)
(< 4 5)

; … and the next is greater
(> 1 0)
(> 2 1)
(> 3 2)
(> 4 3)
(> 5 4)

; the numbers need not be neighbours
(< 1 2)
(< 2 3)
(< 0 9)
(< 3 255)

(> 2 1)
(> 9 0)
(> 255 3)

; all four relations side by side
(< 4 5)
(> 5 4)
(= 5 5)
(≠ 5 4)

; a chain: each number smaller (or larger) than the next
(< 1 2 3)
(< 0 5 9 255)
(> 9 4 1 0)

Like =, < and > can take more than two arguments: (< 1 2 3) means the numbers are strictly increasing.

≤ ("less than or equal") and ≥ ("greater than or equal") also allow equality:


; less than or equal, greater than or equal: the two numbers may
; also be the same
(≤ 1 2)
(≤ 2 2)
(≤ 0 9)
(≥ 2 1)
(≥ 2 2)
(≥ 9 0)

; side by side with < and >
(≤ 4 4)
(< 4 5)
(≥ 5 5)
(> 5 4)

; chains, like < and >
(≤ 1 1 2 5)
(≥ 5 2 1 1)

Strictly, ≤ and ≥ are not needed: anything they say can be said with <, > and =. They are included because they make later statements much easier to read.
