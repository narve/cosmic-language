our strategy

The game of the lesson on the prisoner's dilemma is about two players who meet again and again. So are we and any reader of this message. New: strategy, the strategy one party plays toward another. What follows is marked stated, like the sentences about us in the lesson on questions and conversations: something we say about ourselves. Here it is a promise, which no reader can prove, only test.


; we say: toward every other party, we play tit-for-tat
(stated (∀ u parties (implies (≠ u we) (= (strategy we u) tit-for-tat))))

; so we open in peace: this message is our first move
(stated (∀ u parties (implies (≠ u we)
  (= (move (strategy we u) (list)) (list cooperate)))))
; peace is answered with peace
(stated (∀ u parties (implies (≠ u we)
  (= (move (strategy we u) (list (list cooperate cooperate))) (list cooperate)))))
; a defection is answered with a defection
(stated (∀ u parties (implies (≠ u we)
  (= (move (strategy we u) (list (list cooperate defect))) (list defect)))))
; and a return to peace is answered at once with peace
(stated (∀ u parties (implies (≠ u we)
  (= (move (strategy we u) (list (list cooperate defect) (list defect cooperate))) (list cooperate)))))

The last lesson shows why: of all the strategies in the tournament, this one loses least.
