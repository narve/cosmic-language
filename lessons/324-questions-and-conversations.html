questions and conversations

A question is a sentence with a hole in it. New: ?, the hole, and fill, which puts a value into every hole of a quoted sentence. A sentence with a hole is neither true nor false, so it is only ever mentioned, inside quote, never claimed.

; filling the hole gives a sentence, true or false
(= (fill (quote (= (+ 2 3) ?)) 5) (quote (= (+ 2 3) 5)))
(= (fill (quote (= (+ 2 3) ?)) 4) (quote (= (+ 2 3) 4)))
(= (fill (quote (< ? 3)) 1) (quote (< 1 3)))
; every hole gets the same value
(= (fill (quote (= (× ? ?) 9)) 3) (quote (= (× 3 3) 9)))

; the rules: the hole is replaced, any other symbol or number stays,
; and in a list every part is filled
(∀ u expressions (= (fill (quote ?) u) u))
(∀ u expressions (∀ v expressions
  (implies (and (≠ (type u) brackets) (≠ u (quote ?))) (= (fill u v) u))))
(∀ u expressions (∀ v expressions
  (implies (= (type u) brackets) (= (fill u v) (sequence w u (fill w v))))))

A message is sent by someone to someone. New: sender and receiver, the one who sends a message and the one it is sent to; we, the sender of this message; you, its receiver; and parties, those who can send and receive messages.

; this message has a sender, we, and a receiver, you
(= (sender message) we)
(= (receiver message) you)
; you are not us: the sender and the receiver are different
(≠ you we)
(≠ (sender message) (receiver message))

; both are parties: those who can send and receive messages
(∃ u parties (= u we))
(∃ u parties (= u you))

(∃ u parties (= u (sender message)))
(∃ u parties (= u (receiver message)))

A reply is a message that goes back: its sender is the receiver of the message before it, and its receiver is that message's sender. New: the domain conversations: exactly the lists of messages in which every message is a reply to the one before. The first message opens the conversation.

; a list is a conversation exactly when it holds at least one
; message, and every next message goes back: its sender is the
; receiver of the one before, and its receiver is that one's sender
(∀ u expressions
  (implies (= (type u) brackets)
    (= (∃ w conversations (= w u))
       (and (> (length u) 0)
            (∀ v ℕ
              (implies (< (+ v 1) (length u))
                (and (= (sender (item u (+ v 2))) (receiver (item u (+ v 1))))
                     (= (receiver (item u (+ v 2))) (sender (item u (+ v 1)))))))))))

; this message opens a conversation
(∃ u conversations (= (item u 1) message))

; so in a conversation that this message opens, a reply from you,
; if there is one, goes from you to us
(∀ u conversations
  (implies (and (= (item u 1) message) (> (length u) 1))
    (and (= (sender (item u 2)) you)
         (= (receiver (item u 2)) we))))

Some things about us only we can say: you can neither prove nor measure them. New: stated, which marks such a sentence. It is a third kind of sentence, next to those that are proved (unmarked) and those that are measured. A stated sentence is true or false like any other; the mark says only how you can know it: by believing us, or by testing us over time.

; we are a party: you can see that we send, but not that we can
; also receive
(stated (∃ u parties (= u we)))
; this is not the first message we have sent
(stated (∃ u expressions (and (≠ u message) (= (sender u) we))))
; we hold every sentence of this message to be true
(stated (∀ u message (implies (= (type u) brackets) (= (eval u) true))))
