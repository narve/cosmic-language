geometry

Space has three dimensions. A point, or a step from one point to another, is given by three real numbers: a vector. New: the domain vectors, and dot, which multiplies two vectors item by item and adds up.

; a vector is a list of three items, and each item is a real number
(∀ u vectors (= (length u) 3))
(∀ u vectors (∀ v (list 1 2 3) (∃ w ℝ (= w (item u v)))))
(∃ u vectors (= u (list 1 2 3)))  (∃ u vectors (= u (list 0 0 1)))

; dot: 1 × 4 + 2 × 5 + 3 × 6 = 32
(= (dot (list 1 2 3) (list 4 5 6)) 32)
(= (dot (list 2 0 0) (list 3 0 0)) 6)
(= (dot (list 1 0 0) (list 0 1 0)) 0)
; in general, and its first properties
(∀ u vectors (∀ v vectors
  (= (dot u v)
     (+ (× (item u 1) (item v 1)) (× (item u 2) (item v 2)) (× (item u 3) (item v 3))))))
(∀ u vectors (∀ v vectors (= (dot u v) (dot v u))))
(∀ u vectors (≥ (dot u u) 0))
; stretching one vector by a number stretches the dot by the same number
(∀ u vectors (∀ v vectors (∀ w ℝ
  (= (dot (list (× w (item u 1)) (× w (item u 2)) (× w (item u 3))) v)
     (× w (dot u v))))))

A direction is a vector whose dot with itself is 1: a step of length 1. New: the domain directions, and cos, built from exp, which gives the angle between two directions.

; the directions are exactly the vectors whose dot with themselves is 1
(∀ u directions (∃ v vectors (and (= u v) (= (dot v v) 1))))
(∀ u vectors (implies (= (dot u u) 1) (∃ v directions (= v u))))
(∃ u directions (= u (list 1 0 0)))  (∃ u directions (= u (list 0 (÷ 3 5) (÷ 4 5))))

; cos u is the average of exp(i u) and exp(−i u)
(∀ u ℝ (= (cos u) (÷ (+ (exp (× i u)) (exp (× (neg i) u))) 2)))
(= (cos 0) 1)  (= (cos (÷ π 2)) 0)  (= (cos π) (neg 1))
(∀ u ℝ (≤ (neg 1) (cos u) 1))

; the angle between two directions lies between 0 and π, and its cos is their dot
(∀ u directions (∀ v directions
  (and (≤ 0 (angle u v) π) (= (cos (angle u v)) (dot u v)))))
(= (angle (list 1 0 0) (list 1 0 0)) 0)
(= (angle (list 1 0 0) (list 0 1 0)) (÷ π 2))
(= (angle (list 1 0 0) (list (neg 1) 0 0)) π)

New: cross, which gives a vector at right angles to two others. Which of the two possible ones it gives is a convention, a choice of hand; nothing in this lesson tells a mirror-image reader which hand is meant.

; the cross of the first and second axis is the third, and so on round
(= (cross (list 1 0 0) (list 0 1 0)) (list 0 0 1))
(= (cross (list 0 1 0) (list 0 0 1)) (list 1 0 0))
(= (cross (list 0 0 1) (list 1 0 0)) (list 0 1 0))
; the other way round, the result points the other way
(= (cross (list 0 1 0) (list 1 0 0)) (list 0 0 (neg 1)))

; in general
(∀ u vectors (∀ v vectors
  (= (cross u v)
     (list (− (× (item u 2) (item v 3)) (× (item u 3) (item v 2)))
           (− (× (item u 3) (item v 1)) (× (item u 1) (item v 3)))
           (− (× (item u 1) (item v 2)) (× (item u 2) (item v 1)))))))
; it is at right angles to both
(∀ u vectors (∀ v vectors (and (= (dot (cross u v) u) 0) (= (dot (cross u v) v) 0))))

New: average, the mean of something over all directions, the way mean is the mean over a list. It has the same form as the quantifiers: a variable, what it runs through, and what is averaged.

; the average of 1 is 1
(= (average u directions 1) 1)
; every coordinate is as often positive as negative: its average is 0
(= (average u directions (item u 1)) 0)  (= (average u directions (item u 3)) 0)
(= (average u directions (× (item u 1) (item u 2))) 0)
; the three squares add up to 1, and each has the same share: 1/3
(= (average u directions (^ (item u 1) 2)) (÷ 1 3))
(= (average u directions (+ (^ (item u 1) 2) (^ (item u 2) 2) (^ (item u 3) 2))) 1)
