complex numbers

New: the domain ℂ (complex numbers) and the absolute value abs. Every complex number is a real part plus a multiple of i, and the familiar laws still hold:

sums with i: i + i = 2i, (3 + i) − i = 3, (1 + i) + (2 + i) = 3 + 2i
(= (+ i i) (× 2 i))  (= (− (+ 3 i) i) 3)  (= (+ (+ 1 i) (+ 2 i)) (+ 3 (× 2 i)))
products: (1 + i)² = 2i and (1 + i) × (1 − i) = 2
(= (× (+ 1 i) (+ 1 i)) (× 2 i))  (= (× (+ 1 i) (− 1 i)) 2)  (= (+ (+ 1 i) (− 1 i)) 2)

there is a complex number whose square is neg 1
(∃ u ℂ (= (× u u) (neg 1)))
every complex number is a real number plus a real number times i
(∀ u ℂ (∃ v ℝ (∃ w ℝ (= u (+ v (× w i))))))

the familiar laws still hold
(∀ u ℂ (∀ v ℂ (= (× u v) (× v u))))  (∀ u ℂ (= (+ u 0) u))

<, >, ≤ and ≥ are never used with complex numbers that are not real. Instead, abs measures the distance from 0.

abs is introduced in a new way: besides examples, by a sentence that states what it means in terms of known symbols. The absolute value is never negative, and its square is the sum of the squares of the two parts:

examples first: the distance from 0
(= (abs 3) 3)  (= (abs (neg 3)) 3)  (= (abs 0) 0)  (= (abs i) 1)
3 + 4i is 5 away from 0, because 3² + 4² = 5²
(= (abs (+ 3 (× 4 i))) 5)

never negative
(∀ u ℂ (≥ (abs u) 0))
its square is the sum of the squares of the two parts
(∀ u ℝ (∀ v ℝ (= (^ (abs (+ u (× v i))) 2) (+ (^ u 2) (^ v 2)))))
the absolute value of a square is the square of the absolute value
(∀ u ℂ (= (abs (× u u)) (× (abs u) (abs u))))
