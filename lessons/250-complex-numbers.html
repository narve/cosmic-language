complex numbers

New: the domain ℂ (complex numbers) and the absolute value abs. Every complex number is a real part plus a multiple of i, and the familiar laws still hold:

(∃ u ℂ (= (× u u) (neg 1)))
(∀ u ℂ (∃ v ℝ (∃ w ℝ (= u (+ v (× w i))))))
(= (× (+ 1 i) (+ 1 i)) (× 2 i))  (= (× (+ 1 i) (− 1 i)) 2)  (= (+ (+ 1 i) (− 1 i)) 2)
(∀ u ℂ (∀ v ℂ (= (× u v) (× v u))))  (∀ u ℂ (= (+ u 0) u))

<, >, ≤ and ≥ are never used with complex numbers that are not real. Instead, abs measures the distance from 0.

abs is introduced in a new way: by a sentence that states what it means in terms of known symbols, followed by examples. The absolute value is never negative, and its square is the sum of the squares of the two parts:

never negative
(∀ u ℂ (≥ (abs u) 0))
its square is the sum of the squares of the two parts
(∀ u ℝ (∀ v ℝ (= (^ (abs (+ u (× v i))) 2) (+ (^ u 2) (^ v 2)))))
(= (abs 3) 3)  (= (abs (neg 3)) 3)  (= (abs 0) 0)  (= (abs i) 1)
(= (abs (+ 3 (× 4 i))) 5)
(∀ u ℂ (= (abs (× u u)) (× (abs u) (abs u))))
