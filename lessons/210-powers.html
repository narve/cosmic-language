powers

New symbol: ^, raising to a natural-number power. Like − and ÷ it takes exactly two arguments, and the lesson shows why: grouping matters.

the powers of 2
(= (^ 2 1) 2)  (= (^ 2 2) 4)  (= (^ 2 3) 8)  (= (^ 2 4) 16)  (= (^ 2 5) 32)
a power multiplies the same number again and again: 2³ = 2 × 2 × 2
(= (^ 2 3) (× 2 2 2) 8)  (= (^ 3 2) (× 3 3) 9)  (= (^ 10 2) (× 10 10) 100)
power 1 changes nothing; power 0 gives 1
(= (^ 5 1) 5)  (= (^ 0 5) 0)  (= (^ 5 0) 1)

2⁸ is 256, the first number that needs two digits
(= (^ 2 8) (big 1 0))

grouping matters: (2³)² is 64, but 2 to the power 3² is 512
(= (^ (^ 2 3) 2) 64)  (= (^ 2 (^ 3 2)) (big 2 0))  (≠ (^ (^ 2 3) 2) (^ 2 (^ 3 2)))

the laws: u² = u × u
(∀ u ℝ (= (^ u 2) (× u u)))
one more in the exponent is one more factor
(∀ u ℝ (∀ v ℕ (= (^ u (+ v 1)) (× u (^ u v)))))
adding exponents multiplies the powers
(∀ u ℝ (∀ v ℕ (∀ w ℕ (= (^ u (+ v w)) (× (^ u v) (^ u w))))))
