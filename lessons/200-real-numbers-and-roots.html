real numbers and roots

The rational numbers are not enough to answer simple questions such as "which number squares to 2?". The next three lessons add the real numbers, powers, and the numbers that solve polynomial equations.

New: the domain ℝ (real numbers). Every rational number is real, and the familiar laws still hold:

; 2, 1/3 and neg 5 are real numbers
(∃ u ℝ (= u 2))
(∃ u ℝ (= u (÷ 1 3)))
(∃ u ℝ (= u (neg 5)))

; every rational number is a real number
(∀ u ℚ (∃ v ℝ (= u v)))

; the familiar laws: adding 0 changes nothing, the order of a
; product does not matter
(∀ u ℝ (= (+ u 0) u))
(∀ u ℝ (∀ v ℝ (= (× u v) (× v u))))

ℝ has numbers that ℚ lacks, such as a square root of 2:

; there is a positive real number whose square is 2 …
(∃ u ℝ (and (> u 0) (= (× u u) 2)))
; … but no rational number squares to 2
(∀ u ℚ (≠ (× u u) 2))

; a cube root of 2
(∃ u ℝ (= (× u u u) 2))

; no real number squares to a negative number
(∀ u ℝ (≠ (× u u) (neg 1)))

With variables, the meaning of ≤ and ≥ from the lesson on order can now be stated exactly. The examples there already showed it; saying it again as a law lets the receiver check that reading:

; u ≤ v means: u < v or u = v
(∀ u ℝ (∀ v ℝ (= (≤ u v) (or (< u v) (= u v)))))
; u ≥ v means: u > v or u = v
(∀ u ℝ (∀ v ℝ (= (≥ u v) (or (> u v) (= u v)))))

; squares are never negative
(∀ u ℝ (≥ (× u u) 0))
