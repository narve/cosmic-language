shells and the noble gases

New: the function states, the number of different states an electron can be in at a given level, and noble, a property of some elements. At level n there are 2n² states. Electrons fill these levels like shells.


; the number of states at levels 1, 2, 3 and 4: 2, 8, 18, 32
(observed (= (states hydrogen 1) 2))
(observed (= (states hydrogen 2) 8))
(observed (= (states hydrogen 3) 18))
(observed (= (states hydrogen 4) 32))

; the general law: 2n² states at level n, for every level from 1 up
(observed (∀ u ℕ (implies (≥ u 1) (= (states hydrogen u) (× 2 (^ u 2))))))

Some elements hardly react with anything: the noble gases. Their numbers of protons are where a run of filled shells ends, with the sizes 2, 8, 8, 18, 18, 32:


; helium is a noble gas; so are neon and argon
(observed (noble helium))
(observed (noble neon argon))

; all six of them
(observed (noble helium neon argon krypton xenon radon))

; their numbers of protons: each adds one more filled shell
(= (count proton helium) 2)
(= (count proton neon) (+ 2 8))
(= (count proton argon) (+ 2 8 8))
(= (count proton krypton) (+ 2 8 8 18))
(= (count proton xenon) (+ 2 8 8 18 18))
(= (count proton radon) (+ 2 8 8 18 18 32))

; from one noble gas to the next: the size of the shell that was
; filled
(= (− (count proton neon) (count proton helium)) 8)
(= (− (count proton argon) (count proton neon)) 8)

(= (− (count proton krypton) (count proton argon)) 18)
(= (− (count proton xenon) (count proton krypton)) 18)

(= (− (count proton radon) (count proton xenon)) 32)

The proton counts of helium, neon, argon and krypton were already given in the lesson on elements; they are repeated here to show the pattern. Each size is 2n² for some n: 2 for n = 1, then 8 twice, 18 twice and 32. Element 118 is left out: only a few atoms of it have ever been made, and it may not behave like a noble gas.
