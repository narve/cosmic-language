shells and the noble gases

New: the function states, the number of different states an electron can be in at a given level, and noble, a property of some elements. At level n there are 2n² states. Electrons fill these levels like shells.

(observed (= (states hydrogen 1) 2))
(observed (= (states hydrogen 2) 8))
(observed (= (states hydrogen 3) 18))
(observed (= (states hydrogen 4) 32))
the general law: 2n² states at level n
(observed (∀ u ℕ (implies (≥ u 1) (= (states hydrogen u) (× 2 (^ u 2))))))

Some elements hardly react with anything: the noble gases. Their numbers of protons are where a run of filled shells ends, with the sizes 2, 8, 8, 18, 18, 32:

(observed (noble helium neon argon krypton xenon radon))
(= (count proton helium) 2)
(= (count proton neon) (+ 2 8))
(= (count proton argon) (+ 2 8 8))
(= (count proton krypton) (+ 2 8 8 18))
(= (count proton xenon) (+ 2 8 8 18 18))
(= (count proton radon) (+ 2 8 8 18 18 32))

The proton count of helium was already given in the lesson on elements; it is repeated here to start the pattern. Each size is 2n² for some n: 2 for n = 1, then 8 twice, 18 twice and 32. Element 118 is left out: only a few atoms of it have ever been made, and it may not behave like a noble gas.
