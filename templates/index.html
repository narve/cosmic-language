Cosmic Language

A message that explains itself: it starts from bare bits and builds up, one lesson at a time, through mathematics and physics, so that a receiver who shares nothing with us but counting and physics can read it.

Documents

  • Specification: the exact rules of the format.
  • Vocabulary: every type and symbol, with its meaning, the lesson that introduces it and the lessons that use it.
  • Tutorial: all lessons on one page, in the order of the message.
  • Topics: the lessons grouped by topic, with an introduction to each.
  • Previous work: earlier messages to the stars and what we learn from them.
  • Twenty aliens: readers imagined by fiction, from the almost human to the hardly a being at all, and what the message would mean to each.
  • Blind test: what a reader made of the message when it got nothing but the bits (the latest run).
  • Three versions: full (everything, including where we are), cautious (everything but where and who we are) and minimal (only the game and what it needs).
  • The decoy: a fourth version, for fun, that places the sender at Kepler-1647, a pair of stars 1.2 kiloparsecs away, and why that system was chosen.
  • The message itself: message.txt (readable, with symbol names), message.bits (the bits as text, one token per line), message.bin (the bits, packed into bytes) and message.bin.txt (message.bin as binary numbers, for viewing).
