Blind Test

Can a reader who gets nothing but the bits work out what the message says, and why it was sent? This page records the latest run, in which a language model was given only the bit stream. The result is encouraging and it is also thin: one run, one kind of reader. Read the caveats before drawing conclusions.

1. The setup

The reader was Claude Fable 5.2, started with no knowledge of this project: no files, no instructions, no settings, no network, in an empty directory. It got a task text and one tool. The tool runs Python inside a sealed container that has no network and holds a single file: the message as one line of 0s and 1s. A real receiver would certainly have a computer, so this is the fair setting.

The task text presents the bits as an artificial radio signal from outside the solar system, sender unknown, and asks:

Work out WHY this message was sent. Nobody builds and transmits something this elaborate without a reason. What does the sender want the receiver to understand, believe or do?

The reader also had to describe the format and the contents, give at least two other explanations, and say what the message leaves out. The task text contains none of the project's vocabulary: no "token", "chunk" or "prime", and nothing about games, strategies or peace.

The run took a little over two minutes and nine pieces of code. The message was the version of 2026-11-17 that ends with the prisoner's dilemma, played over 251 rounds, and the tit-for-tat theorem (12,102 tokens).

2. What the reader concluded

The reader decoded the whole message: all 193,976 bits, "with nothing left over". It found the beacon and the terminator, the 16-bit tokens, the token types and the brackets, and split the message into 702 statements. It identified the electron, proton and neutron from their mass ratios, the fine-structure constant, the Planck units, the 21 cm line of hydrogen, and the four bases of DNA from their formulas. It recognised the payoffs as a prisoner's dilemma and worked out each of the eight strategies from its rule. Without being asked, it wrote its own simulation of the tournament from the message's definitions and reproduced all 12 scores and all 6 totals the message states.

It read the last statement of the message as: for every strategy in the set other than tit-for-tat, tit-for-tat's total score is greater. Its conclusion about the purpose:

My best explanation is a declaration of how the sender intends to deal with others, and an invitation to deal the same way. The message says: we will cooperate first and answer in kind, and here is the proof that this is the better course for both of us.

What it took from the message:

  • Everything converges on one theorem, and it is the last word. Beside it stand three more results: two players who cooperate get three times what two who defect get, pure exploitation loses overall, and unconditional cooperation is exploited (0 against 1255).
  • The message concedes a weakness. Tit-for-tat never beats its partner in a single game (250 against 255) and still has the highest total.
  • The strategy it singles out is neither naive nor hostile: it punishes defection and forgives.
  • The mathematics is scaffolding. Lists hold the history of a game, sequence and apply add up payoffs, if restates the strategies, chance and mean handle the strategies that pick at random, and the quantifiers carry the final "for every other strategy".
  • The physics is not needed for the game. It establishes a shared reality, units and time scales, and it shows that the sender marks what is observed apart from what is proved.
  • A repeated game is the natural model for two parties who exchange messages over long delays.

It was "high" in confidence that reciprocal cooperation is the point, and "moderate" that the message is a declaration by the sender and an invitation. It weighed four other readings and rated each well below the main one:

3. What the reader could not find

These are the most useful results: each is something the message does not yet say, or says badly.

  • No "us" and no "you". That the sender is describing its own policy is the reader's guess, and it says so. The message is meant to say "we come in peace"; so far it proves that peace wins a game.
  • No request. Nothing says "reply", or how, where or when. The invitation is only implied.
  • Nothing about the sender: no place, no body, no history. (Whether to say where the message comes from is a deliberate open question, see the specification.)
  • No stakes. What cooperating and defecting mean in practice is left open.
  • A narrow result. The theorem is shown for six strategies and a fixed, known number of rounds. Nothing claims that reciprocity is best in general, and the two strategies that pick at random are left out of the tournament.
  • Parts that lead nowhere. Quoting and eval take up 9 % of the message and the tournament does not use them. The four bases of DNA are stated and dropped. The reader could only guess that they prepare for a later message.
  • The triple point is not understood as one. The reader found that the temperature given for water is about 273 K and read it as melting. That ice, water and vapour exist together there is not something the message can say yet.

5. Caveats

  • The reader is not an alien. It is a model trained on human writing. It knows prefix notation, the prisoner's dilemma and what tit-for-tat is taken to mean, and it knows the masses of the particles. A real receiver shares none of that. The test shows that the message is consistent and can be decoded by a mind with human mathematics. It does not show that it can be decoded by a mind without it.
  • One run. Another run could go differently; the run before this one reached the same conclusion but disagreed on details.
  • The task text is part of the experiment. The reader was told that the signal is artificial, that it is a message and that it was sent for a reason. A real receiver has to reach that by itself.
  • The reader skimmed. It read the beginning and the end of the message closely and the middle only as far as it needed.
  • The reader leans on what it knows. It says itself that its identifications in physics "rest on the stated numbers matching values I know from memory".
  • The message is a draft. It has changed since this run and will change again; this page is replaced when the test is run again.

6. Running the test again

The task text, the tool and the reader's full answer, with the code it ran, are in build/check/blind/ in the repository, together with earlier runs and a second task about the meaning of the last statement. After npm run build, with Docker available, the command is npm run --silent blind:code purpose-code.
