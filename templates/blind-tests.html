Blind Test

Can a reader who gets nothing but the bits work out what the message says? This page records one run, in which a language model was given only the bit stream of the full message, with every bit inverted, and asked to interpret all of it: its format, its vocabulary, what it tells, and its sender. Read the caveats before drawing conclusions.

1. The setup

The reader was Claude Opus 5.5, started with no knowledge of this project: no files, no instructions, no settings, no network, in an empty directory. It got a task text and one tool. The tool runs Python inside a sealed container that has no network and holds a single file: the message as one line of 0s and 1s. A real receiver would certainly have a computer, so this is the fair setting.

Nothing in a stream of bits says which of the two values is 1, so the reader got every bit inverted: the beacon arrives as runs of 0s separated by single 1s, and every word with its bits swapped. The message itself is not changed; only the test does this, so that the reader cannot rely on our choice.

The task text tells the reader nothing about the message except that it is presumably of non-human origin:

A message of presumably non-human origin has been received as a stream of bits. You know nothing else about it. […] Your task: interpret the whole message as completely as you can. Work out its format, every word of its vocabulary if any, and its sender.

It asks for the format, an interpretation from beginning to end, a table of the vocabulary if there is one, what the message tells, what it says about its sender, what seems wrong or unclear, and the reader's confidence. It does not say that the message has words, statements, subjects or a purpose, nor that the bits are inverted.

The message was the full version of 2026-11-19 (306,120 bits): mathematics, logic, conversations and morals, physics, chemistry, astronomy with the Sun, the Galaxy and six pulsars, and the prisoner's dilemma. The run took about seven minutes and eighteen pieces of code.

2. What the reader found

"I decoded the whole file. It is a self-teaching formal language that builds from counting up to physics, chemistry and astronomy, and ends with a game-theory statement."

Format, inverted
It read the beacon as runs of 0s of lengths 1, 2, 3, 5 … 31, and found the inversion from the calibration: "After inverting, the first 12 words are 1, 2, 3, 5 … 31, the same list as the header. This is what fixes the inversion and the 16-bit word length." It took the inversion to apply to the words only, as a mark that sets them off from the beacon; the bits cannot tell that from an inversion of everything.
Checked by program
It wrote its own evaluator: "All 257 statements it could compute came out true and none came out false." It simulated the game and reproduced all 21 numbers it states.
Vocabulary
A meaning for each of 203 symbols, most with high confidence, among them let, eval (with the special forms the message lists), the hole and fill, conversations, at ("lives on / is located at") and velocity.
Knowledge
It compared the measured ranges with values it knows (the particles' mass ratios, the fine-structure constant, the neutron's lifetime, the kaon, the 21 cm line, the triple points, the background radiation) and found them all inside their bounds. It saw what the particle lessons are for: "which particles count as matter, and which way is right-handed, both defined without any shared convention".
The sender
It listed what the sender states about itself, all marked as "its own testimony": that it is a party, that it has sent other messages, that everything in the message is true, that "it lives on B32, on a body that orbits the star B31, and not on the star itself", and that it plays tit-for-tat. It matched the star and the planet to the Sun and the Earth, the central body to Sagittarius A*, and the six pulsars to the Crab, Vela, J0437−4715, B1937+21, B0329+54 and B1919+21.

Its conclusion on the sender: "the message says its sender is on Earth, and it contradicts the premise that it is non-human. Either it is a human-made message, or someone is deliberately posing as being from Earth. The bits can't decide which."

Its own estimate: "about 95 % of the message's literal content".

3. What the reader found wrong or unclear

  • The Sun's orbit around the centre. Kepler's law, with the stated mass of the central body, gives a period of about 30 billion years, not the stated 171–342 million: the Sun's orbit is set by the mass of the whole Galaxy, not by the body at its centre. The message does not say that the Sun orbits the central body under gravity, so there is no contradiction, but the period is misleading.
  • The ends of the background radiation's axes are fixed only indirectly, by the signed components given in the lesson on the Galaxy; no sentence says so.
  • Nitrogen never gets a number of protons; the reader identified it from its code and from ammonia.
  • The antineutrino is never said to be an antiparticle.
  • 1 among the primes of the beacon is "probably deliberate, but it is unusual".
  • Uncertain meanings: stated ("testimony, intention or hope?"), observed (it also wraps laws, not only measured values), stable, the sense of turning, and gases.

4. Caveats

  • The reader is not an alien. It is a model trained on human writing. It knows prefix notation, the prisoner's dilemma and the values of the physical constants, and it says its physics checks rest on "constants from my own memory". The test shows that the message is consistent and can be decoded by a mind with human science, not by a mind without it.
  • One run. Another run could go differently.
  • The task text is part of the experiment. It says little, but it does call the stream a message, and it gives the reader a computer.
  • The message is a draft. It has changed since this run and will change again; this page is replaced when the test is run again.

5. Running the test again

The task texts, the tool and the reader's full answer, with the code it ran, are in build/check/blind/ in the repository. After npm run build, with Docker available, the command is npm run --silent blind interpretation. The bits are inverted unless BLIND_INVERT=0 is set; BLIND_MESSAGE=docs/decoy/message.bin gives the reader the decoy version, and purpose in place of interpretation asks instead why the message was sent.
