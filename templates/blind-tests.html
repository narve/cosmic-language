Blind Test

Can a reader who gets nothing but the bits work out what the message says, and why it was sent? This page records the latest run, in which a language model was given only the bit stream. The result is encouraging and it is also thin: one run, one kind of reader. Read the caveats before drawing conclusions.

1. The setup

The reader was Claude Fable 5.2, started with no knowledge of this project: no files, no instructions, no settings, no network, in an empty directory. It got a task text and one tool. The tool runs Python inside a sealed container that has no network and holds a single file: the message as one line of 0s and 1s. A real receiver would certainly have a computer, so this is the fair setting.

The task text presents the bits as an artificial radio signal from outside the solar system, sender unknown, and asks:

Work out WHY this message was sent. Nobody builds and transmits something this elaborate without a reason. What does the sender want the receiver to understand, believe or do?

The reader also had to describe the format and the contents, give at least two other explanations, and say what the message leaves out. The task text contains none of the project's vocabulary: no "token", "chunk" or "prime", and nothing about games, strategies or peace.

The run took about two minutes and seven pieces of code. The message was the version of 2026-11-17 that ends with the prisoner's dilemma and the tit-for-tat theorem (11,815 tokens).

2. What the reader concluded

The reader decoded all parts of the message. It found the beacon and the terminator, the 16-bit tokens, the token types and the brackets, and split the message into 696 statements. It identified the electron, proton and neutron from their mass ratios, the fine-structure constant, the Planck units, the 21 cm line of hydrogen, and the four bases of DNA from their formulas. It recognised the payoffs as a prisoner's dilemma and worked out each of the eight strategies from its rule. Without being asked, it wrote its own simulation of the tournament from the message's definitions and reproduced every score and total the message states.

It read the last statement of the message as: tit-for-tat's total is strictly greater than that of every other strategy in the set. Its conclusion about the purpose:

The message is an opening move plus a statement of policy. It reads as: "We know contact is a repeated dilemma. We know defection can win a single encounter. Here is a checkable reason why we reciprocate — cooperate first, answer in kind — and why you should too." What it wants from the receiver is a cooperative reply, and an understanding that cooperation will be returned and defection also returned.

How it read the design:

  • The mathematics is scaffolding. Lists hold the history of a game, if and the quantifiers define strategies, apply and sequence add up scores, mean and chance handle the strategies that pick at random.
  • The physics is proof of honesty and competence. The receiver can check 1836, 1/137 and the ratios in the spectrum of hydrogen for itself.
  • observed marks a line between what must be believed on evidence and what can be checked by calculation. The results of the game are of the second kind.
  • The transmission is itself a cooperative move: free, true information, sent unprompted.

It gave this reading about 60 %, and weighed three others:

Other readingIts weightIts main argument against
A deterrent: "we retaliate"15 %The comparisons the message stresses are cooperation over defection, and it shows no threats or capabilities.
A primer or beacon: "we are here, this is what we know"15 %The science stops without astronomy or any description of the sender, and the ending is not science.
Manipulation into cooperating, or something else10 %The message recommends conditional cooperation, which cannot be exploited much. But "the message cannot rule this out".

The reader called these numbers judgement, not measurement.

3. What the reader could not find

These are the most useful results: each is something the message does not yet say, or says badly.

  • No "us" and no "you". Nothing says that the sender plays tit-for-tat, or that the game is about sender and receiver. The reading "a statement of policy" is the reader's inference. The message is meant to say "we come in peace"; so far it proves that peace wins a game.
  • No request. There is no question, no instruction and no channel for a reply.
  • Nothing about the sender: no place, no time, no body, no history. (Whether to say where the message comes from is a deliberate open question, see the specification.)
  • No stakes. Nothing says what cooperating or defecting would be in practice.
  • A narrow result. The theorem holds for six strategies and ten rounds. It is an illustration, not a general proof.
  • Water's temperature is ambiguous. The bounds the message gives allow roughly 230 to 460 K, so the reader could not tell which temperature of water is meant.
  • The rational numbers were read as the integers. What the message says about ℚ did not set it clearly apart.
  • The four large molecules are unexplained. The reader matched their formulas to the bases of DNA, but the message gives no structures or roles and says nothing about life.

4. Caveats

  • The reader is not an alien. It is a model trained on human writing. It knows prefix notation, the prisoner's dilemma and what tit-for-tat is taken to mean, and it knows the masses of the particles. A real receiver shares none of that. The test shows that the message is consistent and can be decoded by a mind with human mathematics. It does not show that it can be decoded by a mind without it.
  • One run. A second run could go differently.
  • The task text is part of the experiment. The reader was told that the signal is artificial, that it is a message and that it was sent for a reason. A real receiver has to reach that by itself.
  • The reader skimmed. It read the beginning and the end of the message closely and the middle only as far as it needed.
  • The reader made mistakes that did not affect its conclusion: a wrong count of tokens in one place, and the integers for the rationals.
  • The message is a draft. It has changed since this run and will change again; this page is replaced when the test is run again.

5. Running the test again

The task text, the tool and the reader's full answer, with the code it ran, are in build/check/blind/ in the repository, together with earlier runs and a second task about the meaning of the last statement. After npm run build, with Docker available, the command is npm run --silent blind:code purpose-code.
