Blind Test

Can a reader who gets nothing but the bits work out what the message says, and why it was sent? This page records one run, in which a language model was given only the bit stream. The result is encouraging and it is also thin: one run, one kind of reader. Read the caveats before drawing conclusions.

1. The setup

The reader was Claude Fable 5.2, started with no knowledge of this project: no files, no instructions, no settings, no network, in an empty directory. It got a task text and one tool. The tool runs Python inside a sealed container that has no network and holds a single file: the message as one line of 0s and 1s. A real receiver would certainly have a computer, so this is the fair setting.

The task text presents the bits as an artificial radio signal from outside the solar system, sender unknown, and asks:

Work out WHY this message was sent. Nobody builds and transmits something this elaborate without a reason. What does the sender want the receiver to understand, believe or do?

The reader also had to describe the format and the contents, give at least two other explanations, and say what the message leaves out. The task text contains none of the project's vocabulary: no "token", "chunk" or "prime", and nothing about games, strategies or peace.

The run took about two and a half minutes and six pieces of code. The message was the version of 2026-11-17 that ends with the prisoner's dilemma, played over 255 rounds, and the tit-for-tat theorem (12,114 tokens).

2. What the reader concluded

The reader decoded the whole message: 194,168 bits, 702 statements, "with nothing left over". It found the beacon and the terminator, the 16-bit tokens, the token types and the brackets. It identified the electron, proton and neutron from their mass ratios, the fine-structure constant, the Planck units, the 21 cm line of hydrogen, and the four bases of DNA from their formulas. It recognised the payoffs as a prisoner's dilemma and worked out each of the eight strategies from its rule. Without being asked, it simulated the tournament from the message's definitions and found all 12 scores and all 6 totals the message states to be correct.

It read the last statement of the message as: for every strategy other than the one that cooperates first and then returns what it receives, that strategy's total is greater. It kept three things apart, as the task asked.

What the message states
Mutual cooperation beats mutual defection. A defector outscores any single partner (259 against 254; 1275 against 0). Even so, the strategy that cooperates first and then returns what it receives has the highest total in the field.
What the reader infers
"The message is an argument for reciprocal cooperation, addressed to a stranger the sender expects to deal with more than once."
What the reader guesses
"The sender is announcing its own policy: open cooperatively, answer in kind. It hopes the receiver adopts the same, most plausibly by answering."

Its evidence for the inference:

  • It is the conclusion, and nothing follows it.
  • It is the only subject that is neither mathematics nor physics, and it takes a seventh of the message.
  • The message is candid that defection wins a single game, which the reader took as "we know the temptation, and here is why not".
  • The mathematics and the physics build the language and show that the sender is competent and truthful, since everything can be checked.

It also described a shift in subject: from necessary truths, to observed facts that carry a marker of their own (observed), to the only topic about choices made by agents.

Its confidence in the purpose was "moderate": that the message argues for reciprocity is well supported, that it is a pledge or an invitation to reply is inference. It weighed four other readings:

3. What the reader could not find

These are the most useful results: each is something the message does not yet say, or says badly.

  • No "we" and no "you", and no statement of which strategy the sender plays. The message is meant to say "we come in peace"; so far it proves that peace wins a game.
  • No request, and no instruction on how or where to reply.
  • No stakes. Nothing says what cooperating or defecting would mean in practice, or what the payoffs correspond to.
  • Nothing about the sender: no location, star, planet, date, picture, body or history. (Whether to say where the message comes from is a deliberate open question, see the specification.) The reader noted that this silence "could be caution, consistent with a sender who has just shown it understands defection".
  • Parts that lead nowhere. The game uses no physics at all, so the reader concluded that the 31 % on nature "is there for another reason". The four large molecules, the temperature of water, the 21 cm line and the two half-lives are never used again; eval and the complex numbers go beyond anything later statements need; the two strategies that pick at random are left out of the tournament.
  • The four bases are only formulas. The reader matched them to DNA, but could not tell whether they reflect shared chemistry, knowledge of the receiver, or something about the message's origin.
  • The triple point is not understood as one. The reader found the temperature of water to be about 273 K and noted that melting and the triple point cannot be told apart.
  • No error correction.

4. Caveats

  • The reader is not an alien. It is a model trained on human writing. It knows prefix notation, the prisoner's dilemma and what tit-for-tat is taken to mean, and it knows the masses of the particles. A real receiver shares none of that. The test shows that the message is consistent and can be decoded by a mind with human mathematics. It does not show that it can be decoded by a mind without it.
  • One run. Another run could go differently.
  • The task text is part of the experiment. The reader was told that the signal is artificial, that it is a message and that it was sent for a reason. A real receiver has to reach that by itself.
  • The reader skimmed. It read the beginning and the end of the message closely and the middle only as far as it needed.
  • The reader leans on what it knows. It says itself that its identifications in physics "rest on the stated numbers matching values I know from memory".
  • The message is a draft. It has changed since this run and will change again; this page is replaced when the test is run again.

5. Running the test again

The task text, the tool and the reader's full answer, with the code it ran, are in build/check/blind/ in the repository. After npm run build, with Docker available, the command is npm run --silent blind purpose.
